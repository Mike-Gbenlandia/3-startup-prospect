Cold storage is a quiet crisis across much of Africa, where unreliable grids spoil food and medicine before they reach people. Nigeria's **Eja-Ice** has raised **$1 million** to attack that problem with solar — building off-grid, zero-diesel cooling from the ground up.


- **Round:** $1 million
- **Investor:** All On (impact investor seeded by Shell)
- **Sector:** CleanTech / cold-chain infrastructure
- **Based in:** Nigeria
- **Products:** Solar freezers, cold rooms, cooling vans & tricycles

## What Eja-Ice does

Eja-Ice designs and assembles 100% off-grid, zero-diesel solar-powered freezers, cold rooms and clean cooling mobility platforms — including refrigerated vans and tricycles. The mission is to extend a reliable cold chain into off-grid and weak-grid coastal and rural areas where diesel is expensive and grid power is unreliable or absent.

## Inside the deal

The $1 million came from **All On**, an independent impact investor seeded by Shell that focuses on off-grid energy in Nigeria. Eja-Ice will use the funding to scale up manufacturing, broaden distribution into underserved rural markets, and expand commercial models including lease-to-own, direct sales and third-party logistics.

## Why it matters

This is the infrastructure-first thesis defining 2026 African funding in miniature: an asset-heavy, hardware business solving a physical problem with clear climate and food-security upside. It also shows how blended and impact capital is stepping in to fund the continent's clean-energy transition at the grassroots.
