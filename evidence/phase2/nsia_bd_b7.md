The Nigeria Sovereign Investment Authority (NSIA) has awarded a combined $275,000 in prizes to Nigerian startups developing solutions in manufacturing, climate and food security, and healthcare, with food processing company Come Again Foods emerging as the overall winner.

[![](https://cdn.businessday.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/300x250-General-Products-.jpg)](https://bit.ly/4BSUQMq)

Come Again Foods, which manufactures packaged snacks from locally sourced agricultural commodities, won a combined $\`100,000 at the fourth edition of the NSIA Prize for Innovation (NPI 4.0), a programme designed to help promising businesses secure funding, strengthen their operations and scale.

Protein Kapital, which produces protein and biofertilisers through insect farming and organic waste processing, placed second with $70,000, while Instant Rad Inc., an artificial intelligence-enabled teleradiology platform connecting hospitals and diagnostic centres with radiologists across Africa, came third with $50,000.

The awards followed the NPI 4.0 Demo Day weekend in Abuja, themed “Building for Impact”, where 12 finalists selected from 6,000 applications pitched their businesses to a panel of experts from Nigeria’s business, investment and innovation sectors.

The $275,000 prize pool comprised $220,000 from the NSIA, $45,000 from Cascador, an entrepreneurship development organisation, and $10,000 from Wema Bank.

Additional awards went to startups across the programme. Eight Medical and Uwana Energy received $15,000 each in Cascador cash prizes, while Sambal Enterprises, Come Again Foods and Ebees Green secured $5,000 each under Cascador’s impact awards. Eight Medical, Smartel Agri Tech and Tryctor also received \`$4,000 each from Wema Bank.

Advertisement

Beyond the cash prizes, eligible winners will have the opportunity to access up to $1.5 million in growth capital through Pula Xcelerator to support business expansion and operational growth.

They will also participate in a fully funded entrepreneurial development programme at the Enterprise Development Centre in Lagos, covering business strategy, leadership, governance, financial management, market expansion and investor readiness.

Advertisement

The expanded support reflects the NSIA’s effort to move beyond recognising innovative ideas to helping businesses attract investment and reach larger markets, at a time when Nigerian startups face funding constraints and the challenge of converting promising solutions into sustainable enterprises.

Aminu Umar-Sadiq, managing director and chief executive officer of NSIA, said the quality of the finalists’ businesses demonstrated the depth of entrepreneurial talent in the country.

Advertisement

“Through the NSIA Prize for Innovation, we are not only recognising promising ideas; we are creating pathways for entrepreneurs to access the capital, capabilities, networks and partnerships needed to build sustainable, scalable businesses,” he said.

The fourth edition builds on previous rounds of the programme, which has attracted more than 20,000 applications across its first three editions from startups in Nigeria’s six geopolitical zones. Participating businesses have covered sectors including financial technology, agriculture, healthcare, education, transportation and logistics.

For NPI 4.0, the NSIA narrowed its focus to manufacturing, climate and food security, and healthcare, sectors it considers important to economic resilience, job creation and sustainable development.

The programme also provides a structured pathway for startups, from application and accelerator training to pitching, funding and post-competition support. Selected businesses undergo capacity-building sessions before shortlisted finalists present their solutions at the Demo Day.

The NSIA said the approach is intended to strengthen business models, improve investment readiness and help entrepreneurs expand operations. Its partnerships with private-sector organisations also widen access to funding and business development opportunities beyond the initial awards.

The latest edition comes as Nigeria seeks to encourage private investment, create jobs and diversify economic activity by supporting businesses that can develop locally relevant solutions. Startups working in food processing, waste conversion and healthcare technology could help address gaps in domestic production, resource efficiency and access to specialist services.

Umar-Sadiq said the programme’s expanded opportunities reinforced the authority’s commitment to supporting innovators whose businesses could deliver lasting economic and social benefits.

“We congratulate all the winners and finalists and look forward to seeing these businesses grow, create jobs and deliver meaningful economic and social impact,” he said.
