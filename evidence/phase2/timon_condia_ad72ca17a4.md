# YC alum Timon gets funding and expands into Kenya after crossing 100,000 users

After reaching 100,000 users, Timon has secured funding from Alliance and launched in Kenya, betting on stablecoins to power cross-border travel payments for Africans.

[![](https://thecondia.com/wp-content/themes/condia/assets/images/google-news-logo.svg)Follow Condia on Google News](https://news.google.com/publications/CAAqKAgKIiJDQklTRXdnTWFnOEtEWFJvWldOdmJtUnBZUzVqYjIwb0FBUAE)

[Funding](https://thecondia.com/category/funding/)By [Justina Salami](https://thecondia.com/author/justina/ "Justina Salami")

![YC alum Timon gets funding and expands into Kenya after crossing 100,000 users](https://thecondia.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto,quality=auto/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Oluwatomi-Ayorinde_Timon-CEO.jpg)Photo: Oluwatomi Ayorinde, CEO and co-founder of Timon

Timon, a travel-finance startup, is expanding into Kenya after surpassing 100,000 users. This comes with an undisclosed investment from Alliance, the crypto and Web3 accelerator.

The growth has been driven largely by referrals and word of mouth. Kenya is now one of Timon’s fastest-growing markets and joins Nigeria, Ghana, and South Africa as one of its four priority markets.

“We don’t expand because the market looks attractive on paper,” said [Tomi Ayorinde](https://thecondia.com/people/tomi-ayorinde/), Timon’s co-founder. “We expand because customers are already there.”

[Timon](https://thecondia.com/timon-card-oluwatomi-ayorinde-fairmoney/) was founded in September 2024 by Ayorinde and [Chizaram Ucheaga](https://thecondia.com/people/chizaram-ucheaga/), both long-time fintech operators. Ayorinde previously founded CrowdForce, a Y Combinator-backed startup whose flagship product, PayForce, [was acquired by FairMoney in 2023](https://thecondia.com/fairmoney-acquires-payforce-by-crowdforce/). Ucheaga previously founded Clymb Technologies, a fintech focused on financial inclusion.

The startup was built on the thesis that young African professionals, particularly travellers, need a dollar card that works anywhere in the world, regardless of local currency. The founders describe it simply as a “financial passport for Africans.”

Timon now bills itself as a stablecoin-powered travel payments platform, a positioning that emerged after it added a feature letting users fund their wallets with stablecoins in response to customer demand.

“We’ve spent the last decade solving how money comes into Africa,” Ayorinde said in a statement seen by Condia. “We think the next major opportunity is helping Africans take their money with them wherever they go.”

That feature has become one of Timon’s biggest growth drivers as nearly 70% of funding activity on the platform now flows through stablecoins. The timing is in line with regional trends. The [International Monetary Fund](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/06/16/stablecoins-in-nigeria) (IMF) reports that Nigeria has accounted for 60% of stablecoin inflows into Sub-Saharan Africa since 2019. The Alliance investment will go toward further building out Timon’s stablecoin infrastructure, customer acquisition, and market expansion.

Timon combines payments, travel, and financial services into a single platform for globally mobile Africans, with customers across 16 countries. The startup has ambitions beyond travel payments.

“We’re building for where financial services are going, not where they’ve been,” Ucheaga said.
