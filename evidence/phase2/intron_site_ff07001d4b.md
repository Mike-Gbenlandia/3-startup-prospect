People often mix languages in one breath: a Kano trader switches between Hausa and English; a Nairobi patient uses Swahili and English; a Lagos colleague blends Yoruba and Pidgin. Global models see this as an error, but Sahara-v2.5 understands it. It’s the first bilingual language-mixing support across 12 African languages, expanding the Swahili-English feature from v2 to the whole continent.

**ABOUT THE SOLUTION**

SautiLedger is a voice bookkeeping app for African market traders
who run their business by talking, not typing. A trader just says
her sale out loud, in whatever mix of Pidgin, Yoruba, and English
feels natural, and the app logs it, reads it back, and can tell her
how much she made that day. It's built with one hard rule: it never
guesses when money is involved — if anything is unclear, it asks
instead of assuming.


**HOW SAHARA WAS USED**

Sahara handles the speech-to-text step that turns a trader's
spoken sale into text the app can safely act on.


**BENCHMARK**

Sahara roughly halved Whisper's error rate on real market speech
and led by an even wider margin on wild code-switched audio. It
also produced far more usable, correctly parsed transactions than
Whisper did — 47% exact versus 13–27%.

**ABOUT THE SOLUTION**

Sauti Yetu helps hospitals communicate with patients who don't
speak the local language, right at triage, when it matters most.
A patient speaks freely in their own language, and the system
figures out what's wrong, how urgent it is, and which department
they need, then fills out the intake form automatically. It even
speaks the health worker's reply back to the patient in their own
language.


**HOW SAHARA WAS USED**

Sahara transcribes what the patient says and pulls out the
clinical details in English for the health worker to read, then
Sahara TTS speaks the worker's reply back to the patient in their
own language — a genuine closed loop on both ends of the conversation.


**BENCHMARK**

Across 27 real clinical-speech clips, Sahara's error rate was
12.6%, roughly a third of Whisper's and Meta MMS's 47–52%.

**ABOUT THE SOLUTION**

GradrAI tackles a real problem in African schools: students often
think and explain best in a mix of local languages and English,
but exams only reward English fluency. GradrAI lets a student

language mix feels natural. It grades the actual thinking, not the
accent or the language used to say it.


**HOW SAHARA WAS USED**

gets graded on content only.
