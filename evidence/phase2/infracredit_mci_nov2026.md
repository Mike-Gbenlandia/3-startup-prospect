## InfraCredit Guarantee Catalyses Local Debt Financing for Magnificent Catalytic Inclusions Limited’s Off-Grid Energy Expansion in Nigeria

- November 20, 2026

_Supported by a UK-funded Climate Finance Blending Facility managed by InfraCredit, the transaction enabled Magnificent to secure local currency funding for its solar mini-grid project._

**Lagos, November, 2026:** InfraCredit, a ‘AAA’-rated specialised infrastructure credit guarantee institution, has provided credit enhancement to Magnificent Catalytic Inclusions Limited, enabling the company to secure local currency financing for the development of four solar-powered mini-grids across Benue and Kogi States, expanding access to reliable electricity for thousands of households and small businesses. The funding was provided through a partnership with the Climate Finance Blending Facility (CFBF), a programme funded by the UK Government.

The project will provide clean and reliable electricity to about 7,032 homes and small businesses in communities that currently have limited access to power. The mini-grids will use solar energy supported by battery storage systems to ensure a steady supply of electricity.

Once completed, the project is expected to create up to 486 jobs, improve livelihoods and help reduce the use of diesel and other polluting energy sources. It is also expected to prevent more than 2,400 tonnes of carbon emissions every year, supporting Nigeria’s efforts to expand access to clean energy.

Including this transaction, the Facility has facilitated approximately ₦27 billion in financing across the projects it supports. Together, these projects are expected to deliver more than 51,000 electricity connections, create over 5,300 jobs and add 5.9 MW of off-grid solar capacity across Nigeria.

The CFBF, managed by InfraCredit, leverages subordinated first-loss capital from the UK’s Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO) and British International Investment (BII), technical assistance from development partners and InfraCredit’s ‘AAA’-rated guarantees to de-risk distributed renewable energy (DRE) projects across Nigeria and mobilise domestic institutional capital from pension funds and insurance firms.

Importantly, the transaction was further supported by the Clean Energy Local Currency Fund (CeF), one of the complementary partners InfraCredit leverages to bring domestic capital into clean energy. Managed by FundCo Capital Managers, CeF is an investment-grade climate infrastructure fund, registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission and certified by the Climate Bonds Initiative, that provides local-currency funding to small and medium-sized clean energy infrastructure supporting Nigeria’s energy transition.

The Project is registered under the World Bank Distributed Access through Renewable Energy Scale-up (DARES) Performance Based Grant Programme administered by the Rural Electrification Agency (REA). The REA ( [https://rea.gov.ng/](https://rea.gov.ng/)) is the official Government agency that promotes, coordinates and provides Rural Electrification across Nigeria.

In August 2022, InfraCredit signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the Rural Electrification Agency (REA) to ease long-term financing constraints for off-grid developers. The partnership is intended to give private-sector mini-grid developers access to credit enhancement and financing, attracting investment into power generation and supply for unserved and underserved communities.

The technical, legal, environmental, and social due diligence costs were supported by funding provided by FSD Africa under a Technical Assistance Agreement with InfraCredit to support first-time issuer costs for eligible projects that can issue climate-aligned local currency infrastructure debt.

**Dr. Paul Healey, Head, Private Sector and Green Growth, at the British High Commission in Nigeria,** **said** **:**

_“Expanding access to reliable electricity is critical to Nigeria’s economic growth, productivity and climate ambitions. The UK-funded Climate Finance Blending Facility, managed by InfraCredit, continues to demonstrate how innovative financing can unlock domestic capital for renewable energy projects that deliver real impact for communities. This transaction will help bring clean power to thousands of people in Benue and Kogi, create jobs and strengthen local economics. The UK remains committed to supporting Nigeria’s journey towards universal energy access, sustainable development and a low carbon future.”_

**Ms. Joy Chiezie, Chief Executive Officer of Magnificent Catalytic Inclusions Limited, said:**

_“This financing is an important step for Magnificent as we expand reliable, clean power in unserved rural communities. InfraCredit’s guarantee, supported by the UK-funded Climate Finance Blending Facility, has unlocked local-currency debt so we can build four solar-hybrid mini-grids in Benue and Kogi States and connect thousands of homes and small businesses._

_“Electricity here is not only light in a house. It is power for shops, processing, clinics and schools — the difference between a community that waits and a community that works. Our goal is to turn energy access into real opportunity: stronger local businesses, better essential services, and more secure livelihoods. We are grateful to InfraCredit and its partners for helping us move this project from plan to construction making a real difference in the communities we serve.”_

_“_ _For me, the importance of these four mini grids is what reliable electricity will make possible for about 7,000 homes and small businesses in Benue and Kogi States. We want families to have dependable power and businesses to be able to grow, create jobs and support livelihoods within their communities._

_I appreciate InfraCredit’s support in helping Magnificent Catalytic Inclusions Limited secure the financing to move these projects forward. Through DARES, our programme to expand renewable energy access, we are combining grant support with partnerships that help developers access long term capital. We remain committed to building on this collaboration and turning financing into electricity that people can use to improve their lives.”_

**Mr. Daniel Mueller, Executive Director and Chief Operating Officer of InfraCredit, said:**

_“We are pleased to back Magnificent as it builds four solar mini-grids in Benue and Kogi States. These communities have had limited access to power, and this project will bring reliable electricity to about 7,000 homes and small businesses, and create up to 486 jobs. It was made possible by three integral pieces working together: first-loss capital from the Climate Finance Blending Facility, local-currency funding from the Clean Energy Local Currency Fund, and InfraCredit’s guarantee. This is the ninth project the Facility has supported, and with over_ _₦_ _243 billion of projects now looking for financing, we expect many more developers to follow.”_

**Note to the Editor**

**About InfraCredit**

InfraCredit (www.infracredit.ng) was established in 2017 as a first-of-its-kind ‘AAA’-rated specialised infrastructure credit guarantee institution, created to support long-term local currency infrastructure financing in Nigeria. InfraCredit’s guarantees act as a catalyst to attract long-term domestic institutional capital from pension funds, insurance firms, and other investors into creditworthy infrastructure projects, thereby deepening Nigeria’s debt capital markets.

InfraCredit is backed by the Nigeria Sovereign Investment Authority, KfW Development Bank, African Development Bank Group, International Finance Corporation, and domestic institutional investors including Leadway Assurance, Access ARM Pensions, and CardinalStone Pensions.

In April 2025, InfraCredit became a publicly listed company and was admitted to trading on the NASD OTC Securities Exchange, marking a significant step in broadening domestic institutional ownership. It maintains the highest domestic financial strength ratings accorded to any financial institution by Agusto & Co., Global Credit Ratings, Intelligence Africa, and Fitch Ratings, which has rated InfraCredit two notches above Nigeria’s sovereign rating. InfraCredit is the subject of two Harvard Business School case studies taught in the HBS MBA programme.

**About Magnificent**

Magnificent Catalytic Inclusions Limited (“Magnificent” or the “Company”) was incorporated in 2021 as a renewable energy company committed to providing solar solutions to unserved and underserved rural communities. To date, the Company has constructed and currently operates three (3) mini-grid projects with a combined PV capacity of 97kWp, providing electricity to approximately 270 connections across Kogi and Enugu States.

**About CFBF**

The Climate Finance Blending Facility is a catalytic facility capitalised with USD21.3 million concessional funding by the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (“FCDO”) and the British International Investment (“BIl”) to mobilise additional funding from development partners to co-finance off-grid clean energy investments alongside InfraCredit’s local currency guarantees in Nigeria.
