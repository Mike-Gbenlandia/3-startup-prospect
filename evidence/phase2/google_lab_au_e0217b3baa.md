# Google's Africa Applied AI Lab Selects Three Nigerian Startups for Inaugural Cohort

Intron, Termii, and Vendy join seven other African companies in a program designed to bridge AI research with market-ready products, offering access to Google's foundational models and technical expertise.

Google's Africa Applied AI Lab Selects Three Nigerian Startups for Inaugural Cohort

The Africa Applied AI Lab operates from the Accra AI Community Centre (AICC) in [Ghana](https://au-startups.com/countries/gh), serving as a platform to translate advanced AI research into practical applications for African businesses and consumers. The program's focus areas include the future of work, knowledge, software development, creativity, and entertainment, with an emphasis on rapid prototyping.

### The Selected Nigerian Startups

[Intron](https://au-startups.com/startups/intron), founded in 2020 by Olakunle Asekun and [Tobi Olatunji](https://au-startups.com/people/tobi-olatunji), develops speech recognition technology. Its systems are designed to understand African accents and code-switching, a common linguistic phenomenon where speakers alternate between languages within a single conversation. The company initially focused on healthcare, deploying clinical speech transcription tools for hospitals, and has since expanded into broader enterprise and government applications, including supporting digital documentation for the Ogun State Judiciary in [Nigeria](https://au-startups.com/countries/ng). Intron has raised $1.6 million in funding across one round, with its latest Seed round closing in July 2024.

[Termii](https://au-startups.com/startups/termii), established in Lagos in 2014 by [Emmanuel Gbolade](https://au-startups.com/people/emmanuel-gbolade) and [Ayomide Awe](https://au-startups.com/people/ayomide-awe), provides messaging and customer verification services. Its infrastructure is used by banks and [fintech](https://au-startups.com/sectors/fintech) companies for one-time passwords, secure logins, and transaction notifications. Termii describes itself as an AI-native reliability infrastructure, processing over 3 billion transactions and reaching more than 16 million end-users globally, according to the company. The company reports that its monthly message volume has grown from one million in its early years to over 400 million today. Termii has raised $6.2 million across seven funding rounds, including a $3.6 million Seed round announced in June 2023.

Vendy, founded in 2022 by Kayode Disu and Peter Ekunkoya, offers a platform for businesses to run storefronts and accept payments within WhatsApp. This allows customers to make purchases without downloading a separate application or leaving the chat interface. Vendy aims to enable offline payments in Africa, helping businesses accept payments from customers without internet access. The company's latest funding was a Seed round in March 2022, led by [Y Combinator](https://au-startups.com/investors/y-combinator).

### Broader Context and Competitive Landscape

[Google](https://au-startups.com/startups/google)'s Africa Applied AI Lab represents a strategic investment in the continent's burgeoning AI ecosystem. Africa currently has over 1,390 AI startups, with Nigeria accounting for the largest share at 323, followed by [South Africa](https://au-startups.com/countries/za) with 321, and [Kenya](https://au-startups.com/countries/ke) with 194. The total disclosed funding for AI startups in Africa stands at $600.5 million, with an average deal size of $2.4 million.

While [Google](https://au-startups.com/investors/google-o3vv)'s program offers significant technical advantages through access to advanced models like Gemini and Gemma, and mentorship from its engineers, the competitive landscape for AI development in Africa is diverse. Companies such as [InstaDeep](https://au-startups.com/startups/instadeep) ( [Tunisia](https://au-startups.com/countries/tn)), which has raised $107 million, and [Aerobotics](https://au-startups.com/startups/aerobotics) (South Africa), with $17 million in funding, demonstrate the existing capacity for AI innovation on the continent. These established players, along with others like [DataProphet](https://au-startups.com/startups/dataprophet) (South Africa) in manufacturing and [RxAll](https://au-startups.com/startups/rxall) (Nigeria) in health tech, indicate a market where local solutions are already gaining traction. The emphasis on building AI infrastructure tailored to African languages, accents, and operational realities, as highlighted by Intron's focus, suggests a recognition of the limitations of global AI systems in local contexts.

### Challenges and Opportunities

The program's structure, which includes support from African venture capital firms like [4DX Ventures](https://au-startups.com/investors/4dx-ventures), [Norrsken22](https://au-startups.com/investors/norrsken22), [Novastar Ventures](https://au-startups.com/startups/novastar-ventures), and [Ventures Platform](https://au-startups.com/startups/ventures-platform), aims to address the challenges startups face in fundraising and market entry. However, the success of these ventures will depend on their ability to navigate operational frictions inherent in African markets, such as intermittent electrical grids, low-bandwidth networks, and diverse linguistic landscapes. The program's focus on applied machine learning over theoretical research suggests an emphasis on solutions that can address immediate, tangible problems.

For operators on the continent, this initiative provides a signal of increasing interest from global technology giants in African-led AI development. The direct access to [Google](https://au-startups.com/investors/google)'s AI models and technical expertise could accelerate product development cycles for the selected startups. However, the long-term impact will hinge on whether these companies can effectively localize and scale their solutions to address the unique infrastructural and market demands across Africa, moving beyond the initial program support to build sustainable businesses. The demo day in December at the Accra AI Community Centre will offer a clearer view of the initial progress made by the cohort.
