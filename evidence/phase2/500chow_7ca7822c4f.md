# Best Food Delivery Apps in Nigeria: Our Findings From Tracking 125 Food Orders

A Liners field study of 125 food delivery orders on 11 apps across Nigeria, from menu to doorstep: delivery times, fees, riders and support.

November 2026•21 min read

![Best Food Delivery Apps in Nigeria: Our Findings From Tracking 125 Food Orders](https://liners.com/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fassets.liners.com%2Fstorage%2Fv1%2Fobject%2Fpublic%2Fmedia%2Fresearch%2Ffood-delivery-apps-nigeria-2026%2Fcover.jpg%3Fv%3D1795209573038&w=3840&q=75)

_"The rider's politeness and calmness needs to be studied."_

The food economy in Nigeria is considerably large, with several layers to it. From farmers, businesses, restaurants, and end consumers, everyone plays a hand.

Yet, there’s a market that lies between restaurants and their end consumers: **the delivery of food.**

In 2025 alone, Nigeria’s online food delivery market size [surpassed $1 billion](https://www.imarcgroup.com/online-food-delivery-market-nigeria?utm_source=liners.com), with projections to reach $2.7 billion by 2034.

But the question remains: is the food delivery market _delivering_? Are users getting value from these delivery apps? What do they experience? And if at all, could it be better?

**The research team at [Liners Africa](https://liners.com/)** sought to answer these questions.

So between September and October 2026, the research team at Liners placed 125 food delivery orders on 11 apps across Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan, Kaduna, Port Harcourt and other cities. They documented each order from the menu to the doorstep: **how long** it took, what it **cost**, **who delivered** it and **what went wrong**.

Surprisingly, what separated a good order from a bad one wasn’t time. It was the rider, the payment and the fees. Read on to see our findings on the best food delivery apps in Nigeria.

## Key Findings: Online Food Delivery in Nigeria at a Glance

1. The **median delivery time of food delivery apps in Nigeria was 30.5 minutes**. 50% of orders arrived within 30 minutes; 13% took more than an hour.
2. **Nearly half of orders were late, but most people didn't mind:** 46% of orders with a quoted time arrived later than the given time. Orders up to 15 minutes late scored the same Net Promoter Score (NPS) as on-time orders: +50.
3. **The delivery rider is the biggest risk:** 21% of riders struggled to find the delivery address. Those orders scored an NPS of -23, against +54 when the rider found the delivery address easily.
4. **Food delivery fees are a fifth of the bill:** the median paid delivery fee was ₦1,000, around 23% of a median total of ₦3,958. 22% of orders had free delivery.
5. **Bank transfer is how users pay for restaurant food delivery:** 60% of orders were paid by transfer, 18% by card and 12% in cash. 13% hit a payment problem.
6. **Most food arrives right:** 93% of orders were complete, packaging scored 4.23 out of 5, and 82% of food arrived hot or warm.
7. **Mistakes cost more than delays:** missing items (NPS -44), app glitches (-20), payment failures (-19) and needing support (-12) all did more damage than a late order.
8. **Promos win recommendations:** promo users were no more satisfied, but they were far more likely to recommend the app (NPS +55 vs +25).
9. **Overall, people are happy:** satisfaction averaged 4.24 out of 5 and the NPS across all 125 orders was +38.

## Why We Tracked Food Delivery Orders in Nigeria

Most articles on " [best food delivery apps in Nigeria](https://liners.com/best/tag/food-and-drinks-delivery/nigeria)" only describe what each app offers. Some still list apps that have left the country. And others only cover apps in Lagos.

We wanted to know what happens across many orders, many apps and more than one city. _How long does delivery take? What does it cost on top of the food? How often does something go wrong, and what happens when it does?_

At Liners, we help people find software built for Africa, with [reviews and alternatives side by side](https://liners.com/compare). This study applies that to the food delivery apps Nigerians use every day.

## How We Ran This Study: 125 Orders, 11 Apps, 83 Testers

- **Who ordered:** 83 Liners testers, each logging their most recent order. Some logged orders on more than one app.
- **Which apps:** [Glovo](https://liners.com/glovo) (25 orders), [500Chow](https://liners.com/500chow) (23), [Chowdeck](https://liners.com/chowdeck) (19), [Heyfood](https://liners.com/heyfood) (13), [Swoop](https://liners.com/swoop) (10), [Foodelo](https://liners.com/foodelo) (8), [Squaay](https://liners.com/squaay) (8), FoodNaija (7), [BukaFinder](https://liners.com/bukafinder) (5), [DownloadChow](https://liners.com/downloadchow) (5) and [Dado Food](https://liners.com/dado-food) (2).
- **When:** 14 September to 30 October 2026, across morning (30%), midday (30%), evening (34%) and night (6%).
- **Where:** about 59% of orders were in Lagos, mostly on the mainland (Ikorodu, Ikotun, Alimosho, Idimu, Akowonjo). The rest came from Abuja, Ibadan, Kaduna, Port Harcourt, Kano, Enugu and Ogun.
- **First orders:** 74% were the tester's first order on that app, so this study mostly shows what a new customer experiences.
- **What we measured:** ease of ordering, menu accuracy, payment, quoted and actual delivery time, tracking, the rider, completeness, packaging, food temperature, customer support, delivery fee, total cost, value, satisfaction and likelihood to recommend.

### 1\. Finding Food on Delivery Apps in Nigeria: Menus, Prices and Mismatches

Liners testers rated food delivery apps 4.01 out of 5 for ease of use, and on 90% of orders, the menu matched what they received, in both price and availability.

Testers on the 10% of orders with a mismatch had specific complaints.

One Chowdeck tester ordered jollof rice and got brown rice. A Glovo tester's soup came with pomo instead of beef. A Squaay tester ordered a Chicken Republic Refuel Max and received it without the coleslaw and drink, at the full price.

16% of orders hit a crash, freeze or error while ordering. The most frustrating errors were about price and availability:

- A Chowdeck tester watched the price rise sharply between the menu and the payment page.
- A Glovo tester said closed restaurants sometimes still take orders, leaving you to wait for a refund.
- A Squaay tester ordered a meal the vendor no longer offered, and the order took more than 24 hours to resolve.
- BukaFinder redirected one tester to a vendor on WhatsApp to see the menu, then left them to agree the delivery fee with the rider.

Testers gave a 15-minute grace window. Inside it, a late order scored as well as an on-time one. Beyond it, NPS fell from +50 to +8.

What testers wanted while they waited was accurate tracking. It was the lowest-rated part of the whole experience (3.96 out of 5), and 14% rated it 1 or 2. A Foodelo tester summed up the worry: an order quoted at 35 minutes took 45 on a weekend, which "makes me weary about when it would get to me on a weekday".

The time of day made a difference too. Midday orders scored best (satisfaction 4.45, NPS +50) and morning orders worst (4.05, +30).

A 77-point swing in NPS. A late rider who finds you is forgiven; a rider who keeps calling for directions is not.

Some address problems came from the app, others from the rider. A Squaay tester reported location issues on the map. A Chowdeck tester's rider ignored the written directions and kept calling to ask for details already in the app.

The riders testers praised had three habits in common:

- **They called at the right moments:** one Glovo rider called once before leaving the restaurant and once on arrival.
- **They stayed polite under pressure:** a Heyfood rider delivered in heavy rain; a Chowdeck rider patiently helped a tester with a delivery code problem.
- **They didn't ask for a tip:** testers on Glovo and Foodelo mentioned this without being asked.

The riders our testers criticised did the opposite: one did not call when running late, and another argued over an order the vendor had got wrong.

### 5\. Completeness, Packaging and Temperature of Food Delivered by Delivery Apps in Nigeria

Most orders arrive complete and well packed. 93% of orders arrived with everything that was ordered, and packaging was the highest-rated part of the experience at 4.23 out of 5.

Temperature is less reliable. 42% of orders arrived hot, 39% warm, 11% at room temperature and 7% cold. Two Chowdeck testers complained the food was cold even though delivery was fast, which suggests food may be leaving the restaurant cold.

Liners testers needed customer support on 21% of orders, and the outcome was all or nothing. Of 26 support cases, 11 were fully resolved and 10 were not resolved at all. Very few fell in between.

On a typical order, the delivery fee was 23% of the total bill.

### 1\. Best Food Delivery App for Returning Customers: Chowdeck

Chowdeck had the highest satisfaction (4.74) and the highest NPS among the larger apps (+58). None of its 19 orders needed support, only one rider struggled with an address, every tester saw the delivery fee before paying, and its packaging scored 4.79 out of 5.

### 2\. Best First-Order Experience: Glovo

Glovo was the most consistent app for new customers. 16 of its 25 orders were first orders, it charged the lowest median delivery fee among apps that charged one (₦400), testers saw that fee upfront on 96% of orders, and none of its orders took longer than an hour. Several first-time testers mentioned free delivery and discounts on most items.

### 3\. Fastest Food Delivery App in Our Study: 500Chow

500Chow delivered fastest, with a median of 20 minutes from order to door. On 15 of its 23 orders, testers also felt it was cheaper than the alternatives. Its weak point was that: 30% of riders struggled to find the address, and testers didn't see the delivery fee _clearly_ on 39% of orders.

### 4\. Cheapest Food Delivery Apps to Try in Nigeria: Swoop and Glovo

Swoop's median delivery fee was zero, because 9 of its 10 orders used a promo. Glovo had the lowest median fee among orders that paid one. Both are good choices for a first order, but check what the fee becomes once the promo ends.

### 5\. Where Food Delivery Orders Went Slowest

Squaay had the slowest orders in the study, with a median of 60 minutes. Four of the five orders with a quoted time arrived late, and testers needed support on 62% of orders. Heyfood also had a long tail: 3 of 11 orders took more than an hour, even though its riders scored well.

### 6\. Chowdeck vs Glovo: What Testers Who Used Both Told Us

## Visible Gaps in Nigerian Food Delivery Apps

The Liners research team set out to time orders. The data also showed three problems that appeared across several apps.

### 1\. Riders Can't Find Addresses Reliably

One in five orders had an address problem, and it was the most common failure in the study. Some came from map pins in the wrong place; others from riders who didn't read the written directions. In a country where many addresses depend on landmarks, a map pin alone is not enough.

### 2\. Some Orders Have No Reference Code

Three Heyfood testers could not find an order reference after paying. One spelled out the risk: "There was no order reference or code, anyone could have collected my order without the rider even knowing it wasn't me." Another had to ask support for the reference after the order arrived.

### 3\. Payments That Leave You Waiting

Bank transfer is the most common way to pay for food in Nigeria, and it is where payments also stall. Testers saw OPay options fail, transfer accounts that would not generate and confirmations slow enough to make them fear their money was lost. 13% of orders had a payment problem, and those orders scored an NPS of -19.

## How to Get a Better Food Delivery Order in Nigeria: A Checklist From 125 Online Orders

1. **Write landmark directions:** add your gate colour, a nearby landmark and a phone number you will answer. Address problems hurt ratings more than anything else.
2. **Order around midday if you can:** midday orders had the highest satisfaction (4.45) in our study.
3. **Check the final price before paying:** compare the total on the payment page with the menu price, including service charges.
4. **Use a first-order promo:** 22% of orders in our study paid no delivery fee, mostly through promos.
5. **Pay by card where it works:** card payers were the most satisfied group in our study (4.59 out of 5).
6. **Screenshot your order reference:** you may need it for support or to confirm the right rider.
7. **Allow 15 minutes beyond the quote:** a quarter of orders took more than 45 minutes.

## The State of Food Delivery in Nigeria in 2026

Food delivery in Nigeria is fast enough for most people. Half of the orders Liners testers placed arrived within 30 minutes, 93% arrived complete, and satisfaction averaged 4.24 out of 5.

Speed is no longer what decides whether someone orders again.

What decides it is everything around the food: a rider who can find the gate, a payment that confirms straight away, and a final price that matches the menu. A rider who struggled with the address turned an NPS of +54 into -23. A missing item turned it negative too. A 10-minute delay wasn’t a deal-breaker.

## Frequently Asked Questions

1\. What is the best food delivery app in Nigeria?▾

In our study of 125 orders, Chowdeck scored highest for satisfaction (4.74 out of 5), mostly among existing users. Glovo gave the most consistent first order, 500Chow was the fastest and Swoop was the cheapest to try thanks to free first-order delivery.

2\. How long does food delivery take in Lagos?▾

The median food delivery in our study took 30.5 minutes. Half of all orders arrived within 30 minutes, and 13% took more than an hour.

3\. How much is the delivery fee on Chowdeck and Glovo?▾

Liners testers paid a median of ₦650 on Chowdeck and ₦400 on Glovo, including promos. Across all apps, the median paid delivery fee was ₦1,000. Fees change with distance, time of day and promotions.

4\. Which food delivery app is cheapest in Nigeria?▾

Swoop was cheapest to try, with free delivery on most first orders. Among apps that charged a fee, Glovo had the lowest median (₦400). 500Chow had the most orders where testers felt they paid less than going direct.

5\. Is Chowdeck better than Glovo?▾

Neither clearly won. Testers who had used both mostly preferred whichever app they had just ordered from. Chowdeck scored higher on satisfaction, packaging and support among its mostly returning users; Glovo had lower fees and a steadier first order.

6\. Can I pay cash on delivery for food in Nigeria?▾

Yes, on some apps. 12% of orders in our study were paid in cash. Bank transfer was the most common method (60%), followed by card (18%).

7\. Which food delivery apps work outside Lagos?▾

Liners testers placed orders in Abuja, Ibadan, Kaduna, Port Harcourt, Kano, Enugu and Ogun as well as Lagos. Availability depends on the app and the area, so check vendor coverage before you sign up.

8\. What should I do if my food delivery order is missing items?▾

Contact support through the app straight away and keep your order reference and photos. In our study, support fixed the issue completely in 11 of 26 cases and not at all in 10.
