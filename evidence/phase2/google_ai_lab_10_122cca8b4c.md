**Google has selected 10 startups for the first cohort of its Google Africa Applied AI Lab, which gives founders early access to Google AI models, technical mentorship and links to venture capital.**

Based at the Accra AI Community Centre (AICC) in Ghana, the [Google Africa Applied AI Lab](https://labs.google/aifuturesfund/africaailab) is managed by the Google AI Futures Fund alongside Google Research. It aims to support founders from across the continent interested in using the latest AI research to address real-world, uniquely African challenges – and, in turn, “build Africa’s first generation of AI-native unicorn startups”.

Google received thousands of entries from startups across more than 45 African countries, and has now selected 10 to take part in the inaugural cohort.

Three of the selected startups are from Nigeria – [Intron](https://intron.io/), which builds speech recognition that understands African accents and code-switching; [Termii](https://termii.com/), which provides customer verification and messaging for financial services; and [Vendy](https://vendy.ng/), which lets businesses run storefronts and take payments inside WhatsApp.

Uganda’s [Crane AI Labs](https://www.craneailabs.com/) builds offline AI tools for health workers and schools on affordable phones; Kenya’s [Satlyt](https://satlyt.ai/) turns satellites into computing nodes that process data in orbit; and Senegal’s [Tolbi](https://www.tolbi.ai/en) combines satellite imagery with voice guidance to help smallholder farmers.

The cohort also includes four pan-African startups. [AethexAI](https://aethexai.com/) provides AI voice agents for banks, telcos and call centres; [Agrails](https://agrails.com/) builds AI-powered insurance infrastructure for farmers, gig workers and small borrowers; [Smile ID](https://usesmileid.com/) uses computer vision for identity verification; and [Viamo](https://viamo.io/) brings generative AI to feature phone users through voice calls in local languages.

Over the coming weeks, the startups will work with Google engineers, researchers and product leaders, building with models such as Gemini and Gemma. They will also get go-to-market support from the lab’s VC partners – 4DX Ventures, Norrsken22, Novastar Ventures and Ventures Platform.

The programme ends with a demo day at the Accra AI Community Centre in December.
