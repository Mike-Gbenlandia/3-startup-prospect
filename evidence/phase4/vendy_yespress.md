# Vendy Built a Checkout _Inside the Chat_

The [Y Combinator](https://yespress.io/y-combinator) W22 startup lets African sellers get paid without their customers ever leaving WhatsApp. Now it wants to be the payment rail for every conversation - human or AI.

Company

Vendy

**fintech**Lagos, NigeriaFounded 2022

Vendy is a Lagos-founded payment infrastructure company that lets businesses accept payments inside the places people already chat - WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram and AI assistants. Sellers spin up a shop in minutes with a catalog, cart, automated invoicing and checkout, and buyers pay without downloading an app or signing up. Started in Y Combinator's W22 batch, Vendy now positions itself as 'agentic payments infrastructure,' offering a single API across cards, bank transfers, mobile money and USSD, and reports more than 20 million transactions processed for around 2,000 merchants.

In most Nigerian markets, a sale already happens in a chat window. A buyer messages a seller on WhatsApp, asks the price, haggles a little, and agrees. Then the awkward part begins: the seller reads out an account number, the buyer switches to a banking app, sends the transfer, screenshots the receipt, and pastes it back into the thread. The seller waits, refreshes, and hopes the money lands. Vendy, a Lagos-founded company from Y Combinator's Winter 2022 batch, looked at that shuffle and asked a narrow question - why does the payment have to leave the conversation at all?

Its answer is a checkout that lives inside the chat. A seller sets up a Vendy shop, loads a catalog, and shares it in WhatsApp. A buyer browses, adds to a cart, gets an automated invoice, and pays - all without downloading an app or creating an account. The friction Vendy removed is small in any single transaction and enormous in aggregate. The company says it has processed more than 20 million transactions for around 2,000 merchants.

## 01The problem hiding in plain sight

Social commerce in Africa was never waiting for someone to invent it. Sellers have run entire businesses out of Instagram grids and WhatsApp broadcast lists for years. What they lacked was a reliable way to collect money inside those channels. Card checkouts assume a website. Payment links bounce customers to a browser tab they may never return from. Bank transfers work, but they leave the seller doing manual reconciliation - matching screenshots to orders, one message at a time.

Vendy's read is that the behavior was already there, and only the money part was broken. That framing matters, because it changes what the product has to do. It does not need to teach anyone a new habit. It needs to make an old habit pay out cleanly.

The infrastructure for chat-based commerce in Africa is still missing. We're building the UPI for Africa, starting on WhatsApp.

\- Kayode Disu, co-founder and CEO

The reference to UPI is deliberate. India's Unified Payments Interface turned a fragmented banking system into near-instant, near-free transfers that ordinary people use dozens of times a day. It won not by being flashy but by being boring, standard, and everywhere. Vendy is betting the same logic applies when the rails run through a conversation instead of a standalone app.

## 02How a Vendy sale actually works

The mechanics are deliberately unremarkable, which is the point. Nothing about paying should feel like a detour.

Anatomy of a chat-based checkout

01Set upSeller builds a shop and catalog in about two minutes.

02BrowseBuyer searches items and adds to cart inside the chat.

03InvoiceAn automated invoice is generated - no manual math.

04PayCard, transfer, mobile money or USSD - one flow.

Under the hood, Vendy connects to the payment methods Nigerians actually use, routing across banks and mobile money providers including Sterling, Kuda, Opay, FCMB, First Bank and MoMo. For the merchant, that fragmentation disappears behind a single integration. For the buyer, the question shifts from "which bank do you use?" to simply "pay."

### The rails Vendy plugs into

- **Banks** Sterling, Kuda, FCMB, First Bank
- **Mobile money** Opay, MoMo
- **Methods** Cards, transfers, USSD, QR - one API
- **Buyer steps** Zero downloads, zero signups

## 03The business model, and why 1% is a statement

Vendy charges roughly 1% per transaction, layered with tiered software subscriptions that run from free up to about $200 a month for heavier merchant tooling. The pricing tells you who the company is built for: small sellers who cannot stomach a fat per-sale cut, and who will happily start at zero and grow into paid tiers as volume climbs.

## 04The boring moat

Anyone can build a payment link. Far fewer can be trusted to move real money at scale. Vendy is a Central Bank of Nigeria-licensed Payment Solution Service Provider, a Meta-approved WhatsApp Business Solution Provider, PCI DSS certified and NDPR compliant. None of that is exciting on a landing page. All of it is hard to replicate, and it is what lets Vendy sit inside WhatsApp officially rather than bolting onto it from the outside.

## 05From marketplace to "agentic" rails

Vendy started life describing itself as "a marketplace on WhatsApp." Today the language on its site is "agentic payments infrastructure." The shift is more than marketing. The company now ships a payment agent it calls UPA, a Virtual Terminal for in-person acceptance on any device, and a checkout tool called Sync for retail merchants - all exposed through a single API and set of SDKs for developers.

The bet embedded in that rebrand is that the next entity clicking "pay" may not be a person at all. As buyers start delegating tasks to AI assistants, someone has to handle the transaction the assistant initiates. Vendy wants to be the layer that settles it, whether the conversation happens in WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram, or an AI chatbot.

Payments as natural as conversations - sell where your customers already are.

\- Vendy company mission

## 06The field it is running in

Vendy is not alone in noticing that African commerce lives in chat. It competes with a growing set of players building checkout into messaging and social apps - Owo, from the fintech Mono, along with Chpter, Xara and Catlog. The broader competition is the status quo itself: the bank-transfer-and-screenshot dance, and mainstream processors that were designed for websites.

What distinguishes Vendy in that pack is less any single feature and more its posture - a licensed, compliance-heavy infrastructure company that treats WhatsApp as its first platform rather than its only one. Its three-city footprint across Lagos, London and Delaware suggests a company thinking about where money is regulated, not just where its users live.

## 07Will it matter

The honest answer is that a lot has to go right. Chat platforms control the rails Vendy runs on, and a policy change from Meta could reshape the business overnight. Competition is real and well-funded. And "agentic" commerce is still more promise than proven market. Vendy's reported metrics are self-published, and its last disclosed raise dates to 2022.

But the underlying observation is durable: people transact where they already talk, and the money should follow them there rather than the other way around. In May 2026 Vendy was named a finalist in the Fintech & Insurtech category at South Summit Madrid, a sign that the pitch travels beyond Lagos. Whether Vendy becomes the standard or simply proves the category, the direction it is pointing - checkout collapsing into conversation - looks less like a bet every year.
