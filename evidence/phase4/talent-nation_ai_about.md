## Twofounders,  one _conviction_

Iyinoluwa Aboyeji and Kesiah Valkin came to Talent Nation from different backgrounds, but with a shared conviction: unlocking the full potential of AI will require more engineering talent, not less.

As AI becomes more powerful and accessible, the ability to put it to work remains scarce. The constraint is increasingly not what the technology can do, but the availability of people who can turn that capability into reliable, commercially useful systems.

We believe the answer is not simply to compete for the same small pool of engineers, but to make that pool dramatically larger.

By identifying and developing exceptional, often overlooked talent, we're building the engineering capacity businesses need to adopt AI at scale — while creating meaningful opportunities for more people to participate in the AI economy.

It's an ambition rooted in two beliefs: that access to AI capability should not be determined by a company's ability to compete for the world's most expensive engineers, and that exceptional people should have the opportunity to build careers at the forefront of technological progress.

That's what _AI for the rest of us_ means to us.
