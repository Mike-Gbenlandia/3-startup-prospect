# What Is a Powershop? Definition, Use Cases, and the Lenol Powershop Model

A Powershop is a designated location where small businesses rent portable power stations as a primary or backup electricity source. This guide defines the concept, details the Lenol Powershop model, and explains the economics of pay-per-use power for SMEs across Africa's informal economy.

May 29, 20268 min readBy Lenol Team

![What Is a Powershop? Definition, Use Cases, and the Lenol Powershop Model](https://www.lenol.co/h/PHOTOS/MW26-4671.webp)

A Powershop is a designated location where small and informal businesses rent portable power stations to use as their primary or backup electricity source. The model removes the upfront cost of owning a generator or solar system and replaces it with a pay-per-use rental arrangement. This guide defines the concept generally, describes the Lenol Powershop in detail, and outlines the economic case for renting power instead of owning a generation asset.

## [What is a Powershop](https://www.lenol.co/blog/guides/what-is-a-powershop\#what-is-a-powershop)

A Powershop is a physical retail outlet that supplies on-demand electricity to small businesses through the rental of portable power stations. Customers visit the Powershop, exchange a depleted power station for a fully charged one, and pay a usage-based fee for the energy consumed. Ownership of the equipment remains with the operator. The customer pays only for power, not for the asset that produces or stores it.

The Powershop model sits within a sector internationally referred to as productive use of energy. The [World Resources Institute](https://www.wri.org/insights/productive-use-renewable-energy-africa) defines productive use as energy consumption that generates economic value through increased productivity, additional revenue, or extended operating hours. Powershops bring this category of infrastructure directly into the commercial environments where small and informal enterprises operate, removing the capital barrier that has historically limited their access to reliable, clean electricity.

In economic terms, a Powershop functions for electricity in a manner analogous to how a filling station functions for fuel. A small business does not need to own a refinery to fuel a generator. With a Powershop, that same business no longer needs to own a generator to access electricity. Both the asset and the maintenance burden are absorbed by the operator. The customer interacts with a finished service. The Powershop is one application of the broader [battery-as-a-service](https://www.lenol.co/blog/guides/what-is-battery-as-a-service) model, in which the operator owns the asset and the customer pays only for the energy they use.

## [The Lenol Powershop](https://www.lenol.co/blog/guides/what-is-a-powershop\#the-lenol-powershop)

The Lenol Powershop is a solar-powered energy station embedded directly within dense commercial markets across urban Africa. Each Powershop is shared clean energy infrastructure operated by Lenol and made available to surrounding SMEs through pay-per-use rental of portable power stations.

A Lenol Powershop consists of four primary components:

- **On-site solar generation.** Photovoltaic capacity sized to the energy demand of the surrounding commercial cluster.
- **A fleet of portable power stations.** High-capacity, swappable units that customers rent on demand.
- **Embedded payments and usage tracking.** Lenol's platform records every rental, monitors battery health, and bills customers on a pay-per-use basis.
- **On-site operations.** Trained staff manage exchanges, customer onboarding, and asset health during commercial hours.

The Lenol Powershop is designed to deliver distributed clean energy access without the upfront ownership costs that have historically excluded informal businesses from reliable power. Every Powershop is intended to support productive economic activity across the surrounding community, reinforcing commerce, extending operating hours, and reducing both fuel expenditure and operating noise within the market.

₦0

Upfront cost to begin using a Lenol Powershop. Customers pay only a small refundable deposit at onboarding, then a usage-based rate per rental.

## [How a Lenol Powershop works](https://www.lenol.co/blog/guides/what-is-a-powershop\#how-a-lenol-powershop-works)

The Lenol Powershop operates on a three-step rental flow.

1. **Onboarding.** A new customer registers at the Powershop and pays a refundable deposit. This is a one-time step. Identification and basic business information are captured to comply with platform requirements.
2. **Exchange.** During each subsequent visit, the customer returns a depleted power station and receives a fully charged unit. The exchange typically takes under two minutes.
3. **Settlement.** The customer pays for the energy consumed during the prior rental. Daily, weekly, and monthly payment options are available to accommodate the cash flow patterns of informal businesses.

Power stations are recharged at the Powershop using on-site solar generation, supplemented by grid power where available. Each unit is monitored continuously through Lenol's platform infrastructure, which tracks battery state of health, optimises charging cycles to extend asset life, and forecasts demand patterns to ensure that every Powershop holds sufficient charged inventory during peak hours.

## [What businesses use Powershops for](https://www.lenol.co/blog/guides/what-is-a-powershop\#what-businesses-use-powershops-for)

The Lenol Powershop is designed to power productive economic activity within commercial markets. Common applications include the following.

- **Refrigeration.** Cold drinks, dairy products, frozen goods, and perishable inventory require continuous cold chain operation. A power station rated for refrigeration loads enables retailers to maintain cold storage without dependence on a fuel-based generator.
- **Tailoring and textile production.** Electric sewing machines, irons, overlock equipment, and embroidery machines benefit from clean, stable power.
- **Personal care services.** Salons and barbershops operate clippers, hair dryers, water heaters, and lighting, each of which requires a reliable power source throughout the working day.
- **Lighting and extended operating hours.** Kiosks, food vendors, and stalls extend their commercial day into the evening, increasing daily revenue.
- **Small appliance loads.** Phone charging services, point-of-sale terminals, fans, and small entertainment equipment are well-suited to power station rental.

The Lenol Powershop is not designed for continuous heavy industrial loads. Businesses requiring sustained welding, large compressor operation, or continuous data centre loads should contact Lenol directly to discuss alternative arrangements.

## [Why Powershops are emerging in 2026](https://www.lenol.co/blog/guides/what-is-a-powershop\#why-powershops-are-emerging-in-2026)

The conditions required for a viable Powershop model have converged within the Nigerian market over the past three years.

**Grid reliability has continued to decline.** The Egbin Power Station experienced complete generation failure on 28 April 2026 ( [Punch](https://punchng.com/egbin-shutdown-grid-fault-paralyse-lagos-power-supply/)). The 132 kV Amuwo Transmission Station has been undergoing rehabilitation since 19 March 2026, with restoration scheduled for 30 July 2026 ( [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/03/18/lagos-community-may-face-four-month-blackout-over-132kv-substation-maintenance/)). These disruptions have driven SMEs across Lagos to seek non-grid power solutions.

**Petrol prices have escalated.** As cited above, the average retail price reached ₦1,281 per litre in May 2026. For low-margin informal businesses, continuous generator operation is no longer financially sustainable.

**Battery technology costs have declined.** Lithium-ion battery prices have fallen for fifteen consecutive years on a per-kilowatt-hour basis. The unit economics of distributed battery infrastructure are now commercially viable at the SME scale.

**Capital is flowing into African climate infrastructure.** Climate technology overtook fintech as the largest recipient of African startup capital in 2025, with climate-focused companies raising more than three times their 2024 totals ( [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/guide/climate-tech-africa-2026-clean-energy-beats-fintech/)). This capital is funding the deployment of distributed energy infrastructure at commercial scale.

Lenol's deployment of Powershops within high-density commercial markets reflects this convergence of market demand, falling unit costs, and available capital.

Businesses interested in transitioning from generator-based power to a Lenol Powershop can begin by [contacting the Lenol team](https://www.lenol.co/get-started). An initial consultation establishes the business's energy requirements, identifies the nearest operational Powershop, and structures a pay-per-use plan aligned with the customer's revenue cycle.

The transition is designed to be incremental. A business does not need to retire its existing generator immediately. Many customers begin by using a Lenol Powershop alongside their generator and progressively reduce fuel dependence as confidence in the rental model grows.
