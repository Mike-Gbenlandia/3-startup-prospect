In September 2026, Africa’s startup scene was very active and reached an important milestone. African startups raised over $2 billion in funding by the end of September, with the potential to reach $3 billion by the end of the year if trends continue.

This funding milestone was achieved earlier in the year compared to previous years, indicating a strong period for the ecosystem, says the [**report**](https://guardian.ng/business-services/african-start-up-funding-hits-2-billion-targets-3bn-in-2026/).

From January to July, African startups raised $1.46 billion, which is a 27% decrease compared to the same period in 2025. Equity funding dropped by 9% to $921 million, and debt funding fell by 44% to $529 million.

Additionally, only 241 companies raised at least $100,000, down from 302 the previous year. The number of active investors also decreased by 22% to 256. While money is still being invested, it’s going to fewer companies. Since 2019, over $25 billion has been invested in more than 2,600 African start-ups that raised at least $100,000.

However, the African Development Bank still [**expect** s](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/investment-africa-2026-q3-signals-q4-opportunities-fady-ismaeel-a1rve/) the continent to grow by around 4.2% in 2026, despite geopolitical uncertainty, tighter financing conditions, and disruptions to global trade.

In September, there were significant funding rounds across sectors including FinTech, mobility, clean energy, AgTech, HealthTech, cybersecurity, semiconductors, and the creator economy.

These deals occurred not only in key hubs such as Egypt, Nigeria, South Africa, and Kenya, but also in Algeria, Cameroon, Malawi, Zambia, Côte d’Ivoire, Uganda, Ghana, and others. Here are the African companies that received funding last month.

Turaco’s model rests on three pillars: distribution through embedded partners such as ASA International, M-KOPA and Safaricom PLC, proprietary underwriting and claims technology, and a focus on affordable microinsurance for low-income households.

## Farm to Feed

**Founded year:** 2021

**Founder(s):** [Claire van Enk](https://ke.linkedin.com/in/claire-van-enk-48314315), [Anouk Boertien](https://nl.linkedin.com/in/anouk-boertien), and [Zara Benosa](https://ke.linkedin.com/in/zara-benosa-12a11425)

Farm to Feed covers sourcing, demand forecasting, sales, logistics, warehousing and payments. Farmers get a clear picture of what buyers need, and they are paid within days rather than weeks. The goal is to cut down on waste and help farmers make more money. In sub-Saharan Africa, about 50% of fruits and vegetables are lost before reaching consumers. Farmers often overproduce or send out lower-quality crops because they don’t know what buyers want. Buyers face challenges dealing with many small suppliers and struggling to get consistent supplies. The company purchases the entire harvest, including imperfect or surplus items.
