# Most Active Investors Backing Nigerian Startups in 2026

Nigerian startups raised $635 million in the first nine months of 2026, but four large rounds supplied most of it. Beneath them, a familiar set of 14 funds writes the first cheques.


by [Bolu Babalola](https://techmoonshot.com/author/bolubabs/ "View all posts by Bolu Babalola")

4 minute read

![](https://techmoonshot.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Nigerian-Startup-Investment-Growth-800x500.webp)
Nigerian Startup Investment Growth

A founder-facing post circulating this week names 14 funds that have backed the most Nigerian startups. It splits them into nine investors based in Nigeria and five based elsewhere but active in the market. For founders raising a first cheque, it works as a shortlist of investors backing Nigerian startups. It also raises a fair question about what “most active” actually measures.

The timing matters. Nigerian startups raised $635 million between January and September 2026, up 157% from $247 million a year earlier, according to Further Africa. Yet four large rounds did most of the lifting. Moove, Flutterwave, Jumia and Yellow Card together accounted for about $440 million of the total.

## Nigeria’s Funding Rebound Sits Above the Pre-Seed Layer

Those headline rounds draw on a different pool of capital than the funds in this list. Most of the 14 investors write pre-seed and seed cheques. A Launch Base Africa review of a year of Nigerian deals found Ventures Platform and Microtraction recurring most often. The same review found no domestic institutional fund leading a priced round above $3 million.

That gap fits a pattern TechMoonshot has tracked. Our October funding outlook described [a market where startup money is back but investors are choosier](https://techmoonshot.com/2026/10/06/tech-opportunities-in-africa-october-2026-startup-money-is-back-but-its-choosier/). Early-stage funds carry the first, riskiest stretch of a startup’s life. Who they are, and how much they write, shapes which companies ever reach a Series A.

## Nine Nigeria-Based Funds Writing Early Cheques

The list below follows the order of the original post, which names Ventures Platform Fund as the most active. It is not a verified ranking. Descriptions of focus and cheque size come from the post unless noted. Deal counts come from Shizune, a directory that tracks disclosed investments, so they carry that tool’s gaps.

1. [Ventures Platform Fund](https://venturesplatform.com/): Backs infrastructure gaps and non-consumption, per the post. Shizune counted 68 Nigerian deals as of July 2026. Paystack, Moniepoint and Nomba appear in its portfolio.
2. [LoftyInc Capital](https://loftyinc.vc/): Invests in African tech from pre-seed. Shizune lists 30 African fintech investments for the fund.
3. [Microtraction](https://www.microtraction.com/): Pre-seed investor writing $100,000 first cheques. Its own site confirms the standard $100,000 for 7% deal. Yele Bademosi and Kwamena Afful founded it in 2017, and Shizune tracks 57 investments across Africa.
4. [Ingressive Capital](https://ingressivecapital.com/): Pre-seed and seed, up to $500,000. Techpoint Africa’s guide puts its fund at $50 million and counted 59 investments as of 2024.
5. [Future Africa](https://future.africa/): Founding investments of $100,000 to $500,000. Iyinoluwa Aboyeji founded the fund, and Shizune tracks 55 African investments.
6. [EchoVC Partners](https://www.echovc.com/): Seed investor in African tech.
7. [Greenhouse Capital](https://www.greenhouse.capital/): Early-stage tech.
8. [Oui Capital](https://ouicapital.vc/): Early-stage African tech. Breet’s directory lists a $30 million Fund II and early backing of TeamApt and Duplo.
9. [Voltron Capital](https://voltron.africa/): First cheques of $20,000 to $100,000. Olumide Soyombo and Abe Choi co-founded it in 2021, and its first fund reportedly backed 53 startups.

## Five Non-Nigerian Funds Active in Nigeria

The second group sits outside Nigeria but keeps showing up in Nigerian cap tables. Several of them write across the continent, which makes them useful for founders planning a regional expansion.

10. [Musha Ventures](http://mushaventures.com/): Early cheques across Africa. Shizune counted 22 Nigerian deals as of July 2026, against 50 across the continent.
11. [Launch Africa Ventures](https://www.launchafrica.vc/): Described in the post as the most active first institutional cheque fund on the continent. Shizune lists it in Mauritius with 86 African investments, ahead of Ventures Platform’s 74.
12. [Kepple Africa Ventures](https://kepple-africa-ventures.com/): Small early cheques across Africa. Shizune also counted 22 Nigerian deals for the fund as of July 2026.
13. [TLcom](https://tlcomcapital.com/): Seed to Series B. Breet lists its Fund II at $154 million, and it joined Ventures Platform in backing insurtech Myka’s pre-seed round in June 2026.
14. [54 Collective](https://54collective.vc/): Venture builder and early investor.

## What Founders Should Check Before Pitching

Start with cheque sizes, because published ranges disagree. One directory puts Ingressive at $100,000 to $500,000. Another lists $200,000 to $400,000, and a third says $50,000 to $150,000. Voltron and Future Africa show similar drift between the post and third-party sources. Confirm the current range with the fund before building a round around it.

Then question the label. “Most active” depends on who counts and what they count. Shizune’s continental table puts Launch Africa first. Condia’s first-half 2026 tracker ranked Lagos-based accelerator Cascador first by activity, a name absent from the post. That does not make the list wrong. It shows that each tracker defines activity differently, whether by deals, rounds or capital. Cascador’s reach also extends into credit, as its [$200 million SME credit backstop with Sterling Bank](https://techmoonshot.com/2026/10/14/cascador-and-sterling-bank-announce-200-million-sme-credit-backstop-fund/) shows.

Finally, watch concentration. When a handful of funds supply most first cheques, founders compete for the same few partners. TechMoonshot reported earlier this year that [new African VC funds were quiet when early-stage founders needed them most](https://techmoonshot.com/2026/05/07/africas-new-vc-funds-are-quiet-when-early-stage-founders-need-them-most/). Fresh local pools, such as [Chui Ventures’ $17.3 million debut fund](https://techmoonshot.com/2025/11/25/chui-ventures-closes-17-3m-debut-fund-powered-by-african-female-executives-and-local-capital/), widen the field, but they remain few.

## What to Watch in the Next Funding Cycle

The post’s author says similar lists for other African countries will follow. If the same funds recur across those lists, it will show how much early-stage capital is pan-African rather than local. That matters for Nigerian founders because pan-African funds can move their attention when a market cools.

For now, the investors backing Nigerian startups at the first-cheque stage are a small, familiar set. The harder question is who writes the next, larger cheque. Until a domestic fund leads rounds above $3 million, Nigerian founders will keep looking abroad for their growth capital.
