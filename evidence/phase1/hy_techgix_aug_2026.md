# African Startups That Raised Funding in August 2026

Over $300M flowed into eight African startups in August alone; here is why these funding stand out

September 4, 2026·5 min read

[![Joseph Chisom Ofonagoro](https://techgix.ng/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fcdn.sanity.io%2Fimages%2Fzr925qxe%2Fproduction%2F84e5d07906585113a469a8cf6dbc9fea80860c95-1944x2592.jpg%3Frect%3D51%2C212%2C1893%2C2380&w=3840&q=75)\\

August was another active month for African startup funding, with large cheques going to companies working in mobility, payments, artificial intelligence, defense technology, agriculture, and renewable energy.

The month produced at least a few standout raises, including a $250 million Series C for Nigerian-founded Moove and a $40 million strategic round for Yellow Card. But the activity was not limited to the continent's better-known startups. Smaller companies such as Verascient, Jem, and Maskh also attracted fresh capital for products aimed at specific problems across African markets.

Here are eight notable African startups and technology companies that raised funding in August 2026.

#### 1\. Moove — $250 million

[Moove's August funding announcement](https://www.mubadala.com/en/news/moove-raises-250-million-at-2-1-billion-valuation-to-scale-the-global-infrastructure-layer-for-autonomous-mobility) made it the biggest startup funding story of the month.

The Lagos-founded mobility company raised $250 million in Series C funding at a $2.1 billion valuation, taking it back into Africa's unicorn club. Mubadala Investment Company led the round, with Woven Capital, Toyota's growth fund, and Ion Pacific serving as co-leads.

Moove began by financing vehicles for ride-hailing drivers but has since expanded into fleet ownership and autonomous mobility. The company says it now operates roughly 42,000 vehicles across 29 cities in 13 countries, while its autonomous vehicle business includes a partnership with Waymo.

The new money will help Moove scale its autonomous vehicle fleet-management business and develop automated facilities, which the company calls “nests”.

For an African startup that started with vehicle financing in Lagos, the move into autonomous mobility represents a major shift in ambition.

#### 2\. Yellow Card — $40 million

[Yellow Card's funding announcement](https://african-startups.com/news/funding/africa-focused-stablecoin-infrastructure-company-yellow-card-raises-40-million/) was another major deal in August.

The stablecoin infrastructure company raised $40 million in strategic funding from SC Ventures, Sony Innovation Fund, Polychain Capital, Blockchain Capital, and other investors.

The round takes Yellow Card's total equity financing above $120 million, according to the company and multiple reports.

Rather than focusing primarily on individual cryptocurrency users, Yellow Card has increasingly built infrastructure for businesses. Its Global USD Accounts allow companies to hold and move dollars, swap stablecoins, manage treasury operations, and send or receive local currencies through domestic payment rails.

The latest funding will support the expansion of those accounts and the company's stablecoin payment infrastructure beyond its established African markets.

#### 3\. Moment — $22 million

Cape Town-based payments company [Moment raised $22 million](https://fintech.global/2026/08/04/moment-closes-22m-round-to-scale-african-payments/) in Series A funding in August.

The round was led by AlphaCode Venture Partners, with continued backing from General Catalyst and MultiChoice and new investment from Canal+.

Moment provides payment collection, billing, and revenue-management tools to businesses operating across African markets. Its platform is designed to integrate multiple payment methods into a single system, helping companies collect both recurring and one-off payments.

The Series A brings the company's total funding to $55 million, with the new capital earmarked for expanding its payment network, improving the platform, and growing across Africa.

#### 4\. Terra Industries — $18 million

[As previously reported](https://techgix.ng/post/terra-industries-raises-usd52m-appoints-former-spacex-executive-as-head-of-strategy), Nigeria's defense technology startup Terra Industries added another $18 million in funding in August, bringing its seed round to $52 million.

Founded in 2024, Terra builds autonomous systems for governments and operators of critical infrastructure. Its products include drones, interceptor systems, sentry towers, and unmanned ground vehicles.

The latest funding is intended to expand manufacturing, including a planned second production facility in Ghana, and to open its first international office in London.

Terra's raise is notable because it shows how African startups are attracting funding in sectors outside the continent's traditional fintech-heavy startup market.

#### 5\. Jem — $8.4 million

South African HR technology company Jem raised $8.4 million in Series A funding, led by Quona Capital.

[Jem's own announcement](https://www.jemhr.com/industry-trends/jem-raises-8-4m-in-series-a-funding-led-by-quona-capital/) confirms the round, while [Disrupt Africa's report](https://disruptafrica.com/2026/08/19/sa-hr-startup-jem-raises-8-4m-series-a-funding-round-to-support-product-expansion/) and [Quona's investment record](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/quona-capital) independently confirm the funding and lead investor.

Jem, formerly known as SmartWage, began by helping workers access their earned wages via WhatsApp. It has since expanded into a broader workforce-management platform covering payslips, onboarding, attendance, leave, employee communication, credit scores, and other services.

The company says its platform is used by more than 200 companies and 250,000 frontline workers.

The new capital will support further product development as Jem targets businesses with large deskless workforces.

#### 6\. Verascient — $1.2 million

Cape Town-based AI startup Verascient raised $1.2 million in a pre-seed round in August.

[Disrupt Africa's funding report](https://disruptafrica.com/2026/08/25/sa-ai-startup-verascient-raises-1-2m-funding-to-help-companies-build-better-teams/) says the round was backed by Founder Collective, Andrena Ventures, Cambridge Enterprise, Summit Ventures, and several angel investors.

Verascient is building AI software that helps organizations to assemble their data across different sources and platforms. Its technology is initially aimed at sectors such as financial services, insurance, and logistics, where businesses often have years of data spread across multiple systems.

The startup plans to use the funding to continue developing its enterprise AI platform.

#### 7\. ThriveAgric — $3.94 million

[Nigerian agritech company](https://techgix.ng/post/7-startups-driving-agronovation-in-nigeria) ThriveAgric raised approximately $3.94 million through its first commercial paper issuance in August.

[ThriveAgric's announcement](https://thriveagric.com/resources/hectare/thrive-agric-records-successful-oversubscribed-debut-with-5-3-billion-commercial-paper-issuance) puts the issuance at **₦5.3 billion**, above its initial ₦5 billion target after the offer was oversubscribed.

As noted in our earlier coverage of the company, it connects farmers with food processors and FMCGs while providing farm management and financing technology.

#### 8\. Maskh — $4 million

Nigerian renewable energy company [Maskh received $4 million](https://african-startups.com/news/funding/all-on-and-energise-africa-invest-4-million-in-maskh-to-expand-solar-mini-grids-in-nigeria/) from All On and Energise Africa in August.

The funding will support the development and deployment of solar-powered mini-grids across 19 communities in Jigawa and Bauchi states, with the project expected to reach almost 100,000 people.

The project will provide electricity to households, businesses, schools, health facilities, and other public institutions that currently lack dependable power.

For Nigeria's startup ecosystem, the deal is a reminder that technology funding is increasingly extending beyond software and fintech into physical infrastructure and energy.

### What August's funding tells us

The biggest raises in August were still concentrated around companies with the potential to operate across multiple markets. Moove, Yellow Card and Moment are all building infrastructure that can serve customers well beyond one African country.

At the same time, the smaller rounds tell a different story. Verascient is betting on enterprise AI, Jem is targeting Africa's large deskless workforce, ThriveAgric is using debt to finance agricultural operations, and Maskh is investing in electricity infrastructure.

That makes August's funding activity broader than a simple list of big fintech cheques.

The month showed investors backing African companies tackling payments and mobility, as well as AI, agriculture, energy, workforce software, and autonomous systems. And while the largest deals attracted most of the attention, some of the more interesting bets were happening much earlier in the company's journey.
