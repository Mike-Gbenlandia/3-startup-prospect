### Kuda cuts 40 jobs as it chases group profitability by 2027

[![](https://c76c7bbc41.mjedge.net/wp-content/uploads/tc/2026/11/greg-hirsch-letting-all-of-you-go-1.gif)](https://techcabal.com/2026/09/28/class-of-2016/) Image Source: Tenor

What do you do when you want to make more money but running your business keeps getting expensive? For Kuda, the Nigerian digital bank, the answer involves a smaller workforce.

**What happened?** Kuda is [cutting about 40 jobs](https://techcabal.com/2026/11/19/kuda-cut-jobs-again/), representing 7.5% of its roughly 500 employees across Nigeria, the UK, South Africa, Canada, Tanzania, Uganda and Ghana. CEO Babs Ogundeyi says the latest redundancies will help the company reach group profitability and meet the capital requirements attached to its Nigerian banking licence.

**Explain like I’m new here:** This is Kuda’s second major restructuring exercise in 2026. In March, the company [laid off hundreds](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/27/kuda-cuts-several-jobs-as-restructuring-hits-core-units-hundreds-feared-affected/) of employees across several teams following a company-wide review. At the time, Kuda said the cuts were not driven by its financial performance but were part of an effort to focus on its core products and customers. Ogundeyi noted that Kuda’s Nigerian operation is already profitable; the challenge is achieving group profitability by 2027.

**Is the profitability plan working?** There are signs of progress. Kuda’s group [losses narrowed](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/11472232) to $5.83 million in 2024, from $35.11 million in 2023. Staff costs also dropped 46% to $6.31 million. However, revenue fell 15% to $18.34 million, partly because of naira depreciation.

In January, the Central Bank of Nigeria [upgraded](https://techcabal.com/2026/01/29/kuda-mfb-national-licence-upgrade/) Kuda’s microfinance bank to a national licence, which requires at least ₦5 billion ($3.7 million) in paid-up capital. The bank must keep that money available to meet regulatory requirements, limiting how freely it can deploy it elsewhere.

### terra Industries invests in South African safety startup

[![](https://c76c7bbc41.mjedge.net/wp-content/uploads/tc/2026/11/news-community-wolf-hero.webp)](https://techcabal.com/category/newsletters/tc-daily/) Image Source: Terra Industries

Terra Industries, the Nigerian defence-tech startup, has [invested](https://www.terraindustries.co/newsroom/community-wolf-investment) in South African public safety startup Community Wolf, leading its seed round. The deal marks Terra’s entry into the public safety market and extends its security technology into a new sector.

**Explain like I’m new here:** Since Terra captured the continent’s attention with its large funding rounds, it has been expanding quickly. In August, it closed [Africa’s largest seed round](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/17/defence-tech-startup-terra-industries-closes-seed-round-at-52-million/) and is building a 34,000-square-foot drone factory in Accra, Ghana, to complement its existing 15,000-square-foot facility in Abuja.

In September, Terra [led a $1 million investment](https://techcabal.com/2026/09/18/terra-industries-leads-1m-round-in-aeon/) in Nigerian startup Aeon, which builds tools to protect networks, cloud systems, and other digital infrastructure. The companies formed a joint venture to pursue government and corporate contracts.

**What does Terra get out of this?** Terra’s existing business focuses on protecting assets such as mines, pipelines, and power plants, but these facilities do not exist in isolation. They sit within or near communities, and nearby residents may notice suspicious activity before a drone does.

Community Wolf gives Terra access to an existing network of residents and security operators who can feed information into its security operations. The companies will explore connecting Community Wolf to Terra’s command centres, allowing verified community reports to appear alongside information collected by drones and sensors.

### Nigeria’s taxman gives crypto companies a compliance deadline

[![](https://c76c7bbc41.mjedge.net/wp-content/uploads/tc/2026/11/rabbit-clock.gif)](https://techcabal.com/category/newsletters/tc-daily/) Image Source: Tenor

Nigeria is tightening oversight of cryptocurrency businesses, and the tax authority has given them less than two weeks to comply with its latest requirements.

The Nigerian Revenue Service (NRS), the country’s taxman, has directed cryptocurrency exchanges and other virtual asset service providers to [register](https://techcabal.com/2026/11/19/nigeria-gives-crypto-firms-tax-deadline/) on its new tax portal by November 30. Companies must also connect their systems to the platform by December 31. Missing either deadline could attract penalties.

**Explain like I’m new here:** Nigeria has spent the past few years trying to bring cryptocurrency trading under clearer regulations. New tax laws [passed in 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/18/nigeria-crypto-tax-law-vasp-penalties-2026/) introduced reporting obligations for virtual asset businesses, while guidelines released in August 2026 set out how certain crypto transactions should be taxed.

The latest directive gives the NRS a system for putting those requirements into practice.

**How it works:** Companies must first submit their business details, Tax Identification Numbers (TINs), and information about their crypto activities. They will then connect their systems to the portal to support transaction reporting, tax assessments, and the payment of applicable taxes.

The rules cover Nigerian and foreign crypto exchanges serving residents, peer-to-peer (P2P) platforms, brokers, and custodial wallet providers. Individual crypto users are not required to register.

**There’s an important distinction:** Registration with the NRS is not permission to operate a crypto business. Companies must still obtain the necessary approvals from regulators such as the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN).

**Zoom out:** The NRS’s latest move is to give the government a more direct way to monitor transactions and enforce existing obligations, including those involving companies based outside Nigeria.

Crypto firms have spent years building businesses around digital assets. Nigeria is now building the infrastructure to tax those businesses and hold them accountable.
