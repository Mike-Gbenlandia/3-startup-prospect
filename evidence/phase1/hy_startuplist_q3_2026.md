African startups raised at least $816M in Q3 2026 (July to September), across 157 rounds in 18 countries. Twenty rounds of $10M or more took $723M of it, 89% of the total.

At least two-thirds of the money in the 20 large rounds was equity. Eleven of them went to asset-finance companies: businesses that buy a car, a motorbike, a phone or a solar system and let the customer pay for it over time.

The figures come from [StartupList Africa’s funding records](https://startuplist.africa/fundings) as of 12 October 2026. Only 99 of the 157 rounds disclosed an amount, so the true total is higher.

The $816M also excludes $506M of infrastructure and other non-startup financings, set out [below](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/q3-2026-african-startup-funding-analysis#two-large-financings-sit-outside-the-startup-total).

## Asset-finance companies took 64% of Q3 2026 funding [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/q3-2026-african-startup-funding-analysis\#asset-finance-companies-took-64-of-q3-2026-funding)

Fourteen asset-finance companies raised $526M, 64% of the quarter’s disclosed total. The largest, [Moove](https://startuplist.africa/startups/moove), leases cars to ride-hailing drivers and alone took 31% of the quarter’s money. [ARC Ride](https://startuplist.africa/startups/arc-ride) and [Spiro](https://startuplist.africa/startups/spiro) do the same with electric motorbikes, [M-KOPA](https://startuplist.africa/startups/m-kopa) and [Mission Mobile](https://startuplist.africa/startups/mission-mobile) sell phones paid off in instalments, and [Odyssey Energy Solutions](https://startuplist.africa/startups/odyssey-energy-solutions) finances solar projects. Eight smaller asset-finance firms, including [valU](https://startuplist.africa/startups/valu), Bridgement, Nawy and SunCulture, account for the rest. Asset finance cuts across sectors: Moove sits in mobility, Odyssey in energy and M-KOPA in fintech.

Moove alone took $250M.](https://blog.startuplist.africa/_next/image?url=%2Fimages%2Fq3-2026-asset-finance-1200.webp&w=3840&q=75)Asset finance is a business model, not an instrument: Moove's $250M was equity, M-KOPA's $30M was debt.

## Large rounds were mostly equity, but outside Moove, asset finance still ran on debt [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/q3-2026-african-startup-funding-analysis\#large-rounds-were-mostly-equity-but-outside-moove-asset-finance-still-ran-on-debt)

Rounds of $10M or more brought in $723M in Q3 2026, and at least $490M of it was equity. Half of that equity was one deal: [Moove](https://startuplist.africa/startups/moove)’s $250M Series C, led by Mubadala, Abu Dhabi’s sovereign investor. Nine companies outside asset finance, led by [Jumia](https://startuplist.africa/startups/jumia) ($50M), [Yellow Card](https://startuplist.africa/startups/yellow-card) ($40M) and [Paymob](https://startuplist.africa/startups/paymob) ($35M), sold another $212M of equity between them.

Asset finance outside Moove looked different. Across rounds of all sizes, those companies raised $180M in debt and $32M in equity. [ARC Ride](https://startuplist.africa/startups/arc-ride) ($33M) and [Mission Mobile](https://startuplist.africa/startups/mission-mobile) ($31M) mixed the two in proportions they did not disclose; Mission Mobile’s facility from DN Invest is backed largely by the phones it finances. [M-KOPA](https://startuplist.africa/startups/m-kopa), [Spiro](https://startuplist.africa/startups/spiro), BioLite, SunCulture, Bridgement, Nawy and valU all borrowed, and Odyssey’s $74M was $47M of debt and $27M of equity.

Most asset-finance businesses still fund their books with debt secured against what customers owe. The quarter’s equity went mainly to Moove and to companies outside asset finance, so Q3 2026 figures are best quoted both with and without Moove.

## Outside Moove, half of large-round money came in deals with a development finance institution [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/q3-2026-african-startup-funding-analysis\#outside-moove-half-of-large-round-money-came-in-deals-with-a-development-finance-institution)

Mubadala also co-led Paymob’s $35M round with the EBRD, so Abu Dhabi’s sovereign investor was behind rounds worth $285M. Across the rest of the market, development finance institutions (DFIs) did much of the anchoring. ## Below $10M, 79 disclosed rounds shared $93M [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/q3-2026-african-startup-funding-analysis\#below-10m-79-disclosed-rounds-shared-93m)

Half of all disclosed rounds were under $550K. The 27 rounds between $1M and $10M, the stage at which companies usually raise a Series A, shared $87M between them, an average of about $3.2M per round; Moove alone raised $250M. The 52 rounds under $1M shared $6M.

The round count runs high because StartupList Africa records grants of any size. Grants accounted for 58 of the 157 rounds, most of them $100K or less, and 14 of them did not disclose an amount. The disclosed grants came to 0.4% of the money.

## Two large financings sit outside the startup total [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/q3-2026-african-startup-funding-analysis\#two-large-financings-sit-outside-the-startup-total)

Two of the quarter’s largest African technology financings went to infrastructure companies and so sit outside the startup total. [WIOCC](https://wioccgroup.net/news/wiocc-group-secures-us-300-million-investment), which runs fibre networks and data centres across Africa, raised $300M from Africa Finance Corporation and Vision Invest. Gotion Power Morocco, owned by the Chinese battery maker Gotion High-Tech, [borrowed €100M ($113.7M)](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/07/24/afdb-approves-e100m-loan-for-morocco-battery-gigafactory/) from the African Development Bank for an electric-vehicle battery plant near Kénitra.

Including these and five smaller financings to non-startups, total Q3 2026 financing comes to $1.32B. Such financings are listed on StartupList Africa but kept out of the startup total so that period-on-period comparisons stay like for like.

## Methodology [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/q3-2026-african-startup-funding-analysis\#methodology)

This analysis covers startup rounds announced between 1 July and 30 September 2026: equity, debt and grants of any size, including listed startups such as Jumia. We count startups founded in Africa, or founded elsewhere to serve African markets. Odyssey Energy Solutions is included under that rule: it is headquartered in the United States and now works across Africa, Asia and Latin America, but it was built to serve African markets first, and we count it under Nigeria, its main African market. Without its $74M round, Q3 would total $742M. Rounds with no disclosed amount count towards the number of rounds but add nothing to the totals. Rounds of $10M or more are each checked against two sources. StartupList Africa’s [Q3 2025 analysis](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/q3-2025-african-startup-funding-analysis) ($988M across 61 deals) used earlier counting rules, so this analysis does not compare the two quarters. Read more about [how StartupList Africa collects and verifies funding data](https://startuplist.africa/methodology).

Our August 2026 newsletter counted rounds of $300K or more and excluded listed companies, so its August total ($344M, since revised to $346.6M after two further confirmations) differs from the monthly figure above.
