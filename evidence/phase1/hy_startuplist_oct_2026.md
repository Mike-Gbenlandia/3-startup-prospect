African startups raised $181.9M in October 2026, across 89 rounds in 15 countries. Three companies took $104M of it, 57% of the month.

We tracked $476.5M of October deals. Most of the difference was one deal that never happened and bonds sold by lenders whose shares trade on a stock exchange. Neither is a startup raising new money, so neither is in our total.

The figures come from [StartupList Africa’s funding records](https://www.startuplist.africa/fundings) as of 6 November 2026. Only 64 of the 89 rounds disclosed an amount, so the true total is higher.

## $294.6M of October’s deals were not startup funding [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/october-2026-african-startup-funding-recap\#2946m-of-octobers-deals-were-not-startup-funding)

Depending on the source, October was anything from a $200M month to a $540M month. Most counts draw on the same deals. The gap is in what each one includes.

![Only 38% of the $476.5M tracked in October 2026 was startup funding. Boxes drawn to scale: $181.9M of startup funding across 89 rounds; OPay $185M, stalled; MNT-Halan $82M, loan-backed bonds; valU $18M, listed lender; and $9.6M from Kuunda and WalaPay.](https://blog.startuplist.africa/_next/image?url=%2Fimages%2Foctober-2026-signal-vs-noise-1200.webp&w=3840&q=75)Every deal we tracked in October, drawn to scale. Only the blue box is in our total.

Five deals make up the $294.6M we left out:

- **OPay, $185M.** Standard Bank agreed to buy a stake of up to $200M at the time of OPay’s New York listing, on condition that the listing went ahead. It did not. OPay halted the IPO on the day trading was due to start (more [below](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/october-2026-african-startup-funding-recap#october-s-big-fintech-money-moved-on-stock-exchanges)). If the listing comes back, we will count what actually closes.
- **MNT-Halan, $82M, and valU, $18M.** Both are Egyptian consumer lenders, and both raised the money by selling bonds backed by the loans they have made to customers. That is how lenders refill their loan books, and they do it repeatedly. valU’s shares already trade on the Egyptian Exchange, and MNT-Halan’s Egyptian arm listed there on 20 October.
- **Kuunda, $5M.** A secondary sale: existing shareholders sold their stake, and the company received no new money.
- **WalaPay, $4.6M.** Reported by some African outlets as an African startup, but the company and its founders are based in North America.

Our total counts new money raised by private African startups, in rounds announced in October, at amounts the companies disclosed. Every round of $10M or more is confirmed by two independent sources, and we do not estimate amounts that were not shared.

## Three companies took 57% of October’s startup funding [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/october-2026-african-startup-funding-recap\#three-companies-took-57-of-octobers-startup-funding)

[ABA Life](https://www.startuplist.africa/startups/aba-life), the healthcare arm of Moroccan technology group ABA Technology, signed a $40M investment agreement with [Admaius Capital Partners](https://www.startuplist.africa/investors/admaius-capital-partners) in late October. The deal still needs approval from Morocco’s Competition Council. We covered it in detail [here](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/aba-life-40m-admaius-capital-partners).

[HealthLeap](https://www.startuplist.africa/startups/healthleap), founded in South Africa and now based in San Francisco, announced $38M on 7 October: a $30M Series A led by Hummingbird Ventures, and an $8M seed round from Sequoia and First Round Capital. [East Africa Foods](https://www.startuplist.africa/startups/east-africa-fruits-co), formerly East Africa Fruits, raised a $26M Series B from FMO, Oikocredit and the Private Infrastructure Development Group. The company reports about $40M in total, including debt from the Schmidt Family Foundation, but that amount has not been disclosed, so we count the $26M.

## October’s big fintech money moved on stock exchanges [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/october-2026-african-startup-funding-recap\#octobers-big-fintech-money-moved-on-stock-exchanges)

Three of Africa’s largest fintechs went to public markets in October. None of the three raised new money for the company in the process.

**Airtel Money listed in London.** Conditional trading began on 9 October, at £1.96 a share, valuing the mobile money business at about £5.3B, roughly $7B. It was London’s largest IPO in five years. The 270M shares on offer, 10% of the company, were sold by existing shareholders for about £529M, so the money went to them rather than to Airtel Money. The business runs in 13 African countries.

**MNT-Halan listed in Cairo.** The Egyptian arm of [MNT-Halan](https://www.startuplist.africa/startups/mnt-halan) began trading on the Egyptian Exchange on 20 October. Its parent sold 20% of the arm, 320M shares at EGP 24.5 each, raising EGP 7.84B. The offer was fully secondary.

**OPay halted its New York listing.** OPay priced 16M shares at $19, the bottom of its range, for an offering worth about $304M that valued the company at more than $4B. It then halted the IPO on 30 October, the day its shares were due to start trading on the New York Stock Exchange, citing an inquiry by a regulator in a jurisdiction it described as immaterial to its core business. Bloomberg reported that the inquiry came from Chinese authorities. OPay said its Nigerian operations were unaffected. Standard Bank’s planned stake fell away with the listing.

## Other things that moved in October [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/october-2026-african-startup-funding-recap\#other-things-that-moved-in-october)

**Klump shut down.** The Nigerian buy-now-pay-later startup closed after five years, its co-founders Celestine Omin and Olufunbi Falayi said on 25 October. They blamed bank integrations that took six to 18 months each, and their failure to raise the debt they needed to grow. Klump had raised about $1.2M, from backers including Seedcamp and Magic Fund, and turned down an acquisition offer earlier in 2026.

**LekkeSlaap was sold to South African investors.** A consortium led by Bellair Road Investment Partners and Janic Capital, with Investec and two family offices, bought the accommodation booking platform, announced on 8 October. Co-founders Jonathan Womersley and Marcel van de Ghinste exited after 17 years. The platform lists more than 40,000 properties across Southern Africa. Terms were not disclosed.

**Kenya raised the bar for digital lenders.** New Central Bank of Kenya rules for non-deposit-taking lenders, published on 29 September and reported in early October, lift the annual licence fee from KES 20,000 to KES 500,000. Lenders now need the central bank’s written approval before launching a new credit product or changing an interest rate. Existing lenders have until 29 March 2027 to apply under the new regime.

**Google Pay launched in Nigeria.** Android users can now tap to pay with cards from FirstBank, GTBank, Zenith Bank and UBA, through Mastercard and Interswitch. UBA also supports Visa prepaid cards.

## Frequently asked questions about October 2026 African startup funding [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/october-2026-african-startup-funding-recap\#frequently-asked-questions-about-october-2026-african-startup-funding)

### How much did African startups raise in October 2026?

African startups raised $181.9M across 89 rounds in October 2026, according to StartupList Africa. Of those rounds, 64 disclosed an amount.

### Why do other October 2026 totals differ?

StartupList Africa tracked $476.5M of October deals but counted only new money raised by private African startups. It left out OPay’s $185M stake sale, which depended on an IPO now on hold, $100M of bonds sold by listed lenders MNT-Halan and valU, Kuunda’s $5M secondary sale and WalaPay’s $4.6M, because WalaPay is not an African company.

### What was the largest African startup round in October 2026?

ABA Life’s $40M investment agreement with Admaius Capital Partners, signed in late October and still subject to approval from Morocco’s Competition Council. HealthLeap raised $38M and East Africa Foods $26M.

### Which sector raised the most in October 2026?

Health, with $79.4M across 12 rounds, or 44% of the month. Energy and climate followed with $40.4M (22%). Fintech startups raised $3.5M (2%).

### How much of October 2026 startup funding was debt?

$47.3M, or 26%, across five rounds. Every one came from a development or impact lender: REPP, Finnfund, the EBRD, British International Investment and Hivos-Triodos Fonds.

## Methodology [\#](https://blog.startuplist.africa/articles/october-2026-african-startup-funding-recap\#methodology)

This analysis covers startup rounds announced between 1 and 31 October 2026: equity, debt and grants of any size, at amounts the companies disclosed. Every round of $10M or more is checked against two independent sources. We count startups founded in Africa or with their main operations or market there, including African-founded companies headquartered abroad such as HealthLeap.

October’s total leaves out financings by listed companies, deals that depend on a future IPO, secondary share sales and companies with no African base. Our Q3 2026 analysis counted listed startups such as Jumia and valU, so the two figures are not directly comparable.

The October rounds can be explored on [StartupList Africa](https://www.startuplist.africa/fundings), with investors, sources and each company’s funding history.
