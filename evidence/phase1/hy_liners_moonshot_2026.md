# Every African Company that was Present at Moonshot 2026 and the Problems They're Solving

Almost 100 African companies were at Moonshot 2026 in Lagos. We went through every one: the problem it solves, what it has built and how it is shaping African tech.

October 2026•29 min read

![Every African Company that was Present at Moonshot 2026 and the Problems They're Solving](https://liners.com/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fassets.liners.com%2Fstorage%2Fv1%2Fobject%2Fpublic%2Fmedia%2Fresearch%2Fmoonshot-2026-african-companies%2Fcover.jpg%3Fv%3D1793545663997&w=3840&q=75)

Two exhibition halls at the National Theatre in Lagos ran out of space before Moonshot 2026 was over.

Over 7,500 people came through TechCabal's flagship conference on 28 and 29 October, and almost 100 African companies set up a booth, sponsored a stage, pitched on TC Battlefield or ran part of the experience.

So we went through every single one of them.

We checked their websites, their apps, their social pages and the press around them, then answered two questions for each company: what problem are you solving, and what have you built to solve it? This is that list, grouped by sector, with the startups that pitched for prize money at the end.

## TL;DR

- Moonshot 2026 brought together almost 100 African companies at the National Theatre, Lagos, with over 7,500 attendees across two days.
- Payments and cross-border money is the biggest cluster: over 20 companies are building around how money moves into, out of and across Africa.
- Crypto and stablecoin platforms are mostly tools for cashing out to Naira or holding dollars, not trading.
- AI products on show were built for local conditions: African accents, the Nigerian curriculum, Nigerian tax rules and WhatsApp.
- Acecore won TC Battlefield and $10,000 with locally made solar battery systems; Supplya placed second and Navidyn third.
- Liners, the home of African software, lists most of these companies with their reviews, alternatives and funding history.

## How We Built This List of Moonshot 2026 Companies

This list covers every African company we could confirm at Moonshot 2026: sponsors, exhibitors, partners, Deal Room startups, the develoPPP showcase, the Deep Tech pavilion and the TC Battlefield finalists named in [TechCabal's write-up](https://techcabal.beehiiv.com/p/acecore-wins-10-000-at-moonshot?utm_source=liners.com).

For each company, we looked at three things:

- **The problem:** the specific pain the business exists to fix.
- **The solution:** what they've built around it, by product name where possible.
- **The proof:** numbers that show traction (funding, users, licences, markets), only where a public source confirms them.

Companies are grouped by what they do, not by sponsorship tier, so a Platinum sponsor and a first-time exhibitor can appear side by side if they solve the same problem.

## Payments and Cross-Border Money Companies at Moonshot 2026

Payments was the biggest cluster at Moonshot 2026, with over 20 companies. They split into two camps: the rails that let Nigerians pay each other (switches, gateways, POS and mobile money) and the companies fixing how money moves _across_ borders, from freelancers getting paid in dollars to importers settling invoices in China.

### 1\. Flutterwave (Nigeria / USA)

![Flutterwave product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/flutterwave/screenshot.webp)

[Flutterwave](https://liners.com/flutterwave) is a payments company that lets businesses accept and send money across Africa through one API.

**The problem:** A business selling in several African countries has to deal with different currencies, card networks, bank transfers and mobile money wallets in each market.

**The solution:** A single payment gateway, plus payouts, Send (remittances), Swap and card issuing. Flutterwave has processed over $16 billion and serves 900,000+ businesses.

### 2\. Paystack (Nigeria)

![Paystack product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/paystack/screenshot.webp)

[Paystack](https://liners.com/paystack) is an online payment gateway, founded in 2015 by Shola Akinlade and Ezra Olubi and acquired by Stripe in 2020.

**The problem:** Before gateways like Paystack, Nigerian businesses struggled to accept card and bank payments online, and many sales ended at checkout.

**The solution:** Checkout via cards, bank transfers, USSD and mobile money, plus transfer APIs. Paystack powers 200,000+ businesses.

### 3\. Interswitch (Nigeria)

![Interswitch product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/interswitchgroup/screenshot.png)

[Interswitch](https://liners.com/interswitch) is the payment switch that connects Nigerian banks, founded in 2002 by Mitchell Elegbe.

**The problem:** In the early 2000s, Nigerian banks weren't connected to each other, so a customer of one bank couldn't use another bank's ATM or pay a merchant electronically.

**The solution:** The shared switch behind ATM, POS and online payments, the Verve card scheme and Quickteller. Interswitch is live in Nigeria, Kenya and Uganda.

### 4\. CoralPay (Nigeria)

![CoralPay product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/coralpay/screenshot.webp)

[CoralPay](https://liners.com/coralpay) is a payment processor known for C'Gate, Nigeria's first interoperable USSD payment platform.

**The problem:** Customers without a debit card or mobile data had no way to pay a merchant directly from their bank account.

**The solution:** USSD payments that work across POS, web and ATMs, adopted by banks including Zenith, GTBank and Access.

### 5\. Nitroswitch (Nigeria)

[Nitroswitch](https://nitroswitch.com/?utm_source=liners.com) is a licensed value-added services (VAS) aggregator that runs messaging and payment infrastructure for businesses and telcos.

**The problem:** Businesses that want to collect payments or send messages over USSD, SMS and voice need direct integrations with every mobile network.

**The solution:** One VAS gateway for messaging, payment processing, gaming and lottery platforms.

### 6\. OPay (Nigeria)

![OPay product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/opayweb/screenshot.webp)

[OPay](https://liners.com/opay) is a mobile money and banking app with over 50 million users in Nigeria.

**The problem:** Bank transfer fees, failed transactions and low savings interest push millions of Nigerians toward cash.

**The solution:** Free transfers, bill payments, debit cards and OWealth, a savings wallet that pays interest daily.

### 7\. Paga (Nigeria)

![Paga product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/mypaga/screenshot.webp)

[Paga](https://liners.com/paga) is a mobile money platform founded in 2009 by Tayo Oviosu.

**The problem:** Millions of Nigerians live far from a bank branch, so paying bills or sending money means travelling with cash.

**The solution:** A mobile money app, a network of 120,000+ agents, Doroki for small businesses and Paga Engine for enterprises. Paga has reported 19 million+ users.

### 8\. Nomba (Nigeria)

![Nomba product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/kudi/screenshot.webp)

[Nomba](https://liners.com/nomba) is a business banking and payments platform for merchants.

**The problem:** Retailers lose sales when card payments fail at the till, and they need separate tools for accounts, invoicing and loans.

**The solution:** POS terminals, business accounts, checkout, invoicing, loans and treasury in one place. Nomba says 600,000+ businesses use it, processing ₦7 trillion+ a month.

### 9\. Grey (Nigeria / USA), Headline Sponsor

![Grey product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/grey/screenshot.webp)

You're a Lagos-based designer with a client in London. Getting that £2,000 home can take days and lose a chunk to fees.

[Grey](https://liners.com/grey) gives Africans foreign currency accounts in USD, GBP and EUR. It was founded in 2020 by Idorenyin Obong and Femi Aghedo and went through Y Combinator (W22).

**The problem:** African freelancers and remote workers paid by foreign clients lose money to high fees and poor exchange rates, and many can't open a foreign account at a local bank.

**The solution:** Foreign accounts, currency exchange, virtual cards and savings in one app. Grey passed 1 million users in 2024.

### 10\. Raenest (Nigeria)

![Raenest product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/raenest/screenshot.webp)

[Raenest](https://liners.com/raenest) offers global accounts for freelancers and businesses, and is licensed by the CBN as an international money transfer operator.

**The problem:** Payments from foreign clients and employers take days to arrive and lose value to fees along the way.

**The solution:** USD, GBP, EUR and stablecoin accounts, Visa cards, invoicing and US stock trading. Raenest raised an $11 million Series A led by QED and has processed over $1 billion.

### 11\. Juicyway (Nigeria)

![Juicyway product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/juicyway/screenshot.webp)

[Juicyway](https://liners.com/juicyway) is a licensed cross-border payments and FX platform for businesses.

**The problem:** Nigerian businesses that need dollars to pay foreign suppliers often can't get them quickly or legally.

**The solution:** An FX marketplace that matches Naira sellers with licensed FX suppliers, plus multi-currency accounts and payouts. Juicyway has processed $1.3 billion for 4,000+ customers.

### 12\. Esca Finance (Nigeria / Ireland)

![Esca Finance product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/esca/screenshot.webp)

[Esca Finance](https://liners.com/esca) is an FX hedging and treasury platform, founded by Shalom Osiadi.

**The problem:** Businesses that earn in Naira lose value every day they wait to convert to dollars or euros.

**The solution:** Automatic conversion sweeps, forward contracts and interest-bearing accounts. Esca processes $75 million to $120 million a month for clients including NALA and MoneyGram.

### 13\. Bluebulb (Nigeria / UK)

![Bluebulb product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/bluebulb/screenshot.webp)

[Bluebulb](https://liners.com/bluebulb) handles cross-border payments, FX and treasury for African corporates.

**The problem:** African companies paying foreign suppliers juggle several payment platforms and pay high fees on each transfer.

**The solution:** Pay-ins, payouts, FX and collections to the UK, US, EU and China, plus Orbita, a self-service treasury dashboard. Its clients include Oando and Nigerian Breweries.

### 14\. HoneyCoin (Kenya)

![HoneyCoin product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/honeycoin/screenshot.png)

[HoneyCoin](https://liners.com/honeycoin) is a Nairobi-based B2B payments company that uses stablecoins to settle cross-border transactions.

**The problem:** Sending money between African countries through correspondent banks takes days and costs a lot.

**The solution:** Wallets, virtual accounts, OTC and bulk payouts on stablecoin rails. HoneyCoin raised a $4.9 million seed and moves about $150 million a month for 350 enterprise clients.

### 15\. Glo Currency (Nigeria / UK)

![Glo Currency product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/content/inline/79fe0a44-9eaf-4d95-8f03-0bfc2536a6ef.webp?v=1793547789162)

[Glo Currency](https://glocurrency.com/?utm_source=liners.com) is an FCA-authorised FX and cross-border payments provider, founded in 2013 by Jamilah Alli.

**The problem:** Businesses that use informal FX middlemen risk delays, frozen funds or losing the money entirely.

**The solution:** Regulated multi-currency accounts, transfers and a business API, with same-day transfers to 196+ countries.

### 16\. Nairagram

![Nairagram product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/nairagramltd/screenshot.webp)

[Nairagram](https://liners.com/nairagram) is a payout network that delivers money to bank accounts, mobile wallets and cash points across Africa.

**The problem:** Money sent from abroad often can't reach recipients who don't have a bank account in a major city.

**The solution:** Bank deposits, mobile wallet payouts in 30+ countries and cash pickup at 10,000+ locations. Nairagram processes over $2 billion a year across 37 African countries.

### 17\. AfriChange (Canada / Africa)

![AfriChange product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/africhange/screenshot.webp)

[AfriChange](https://liners.com/africhange) is a FINTRAC-registered remittance app for sending money from Canada to Africa.

**The problem:** Sending Canadian dollars home to Nigeria, Ghana or Kenya is slow and expensive through banks.

**The solution:** CAD to Naira, cedi, shilling and CFA transfers funded via Interac.

### 18\. Timon (Nigeria)

![Timon product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/usetimon/screenshot.webp)

Your card gets declined at a hotel in Accra. Now what?

[Timon](https://liners.com/timon) is a travel payments app for Africans, launched in 2024.

**The problem:** African bank cards are often declined abroad for hotels, transport and bookings.

**The solution:** A USD Visa card, global wallets, eSIMs and flight and hotel bookings. Timon has about 100,000 users across 16 African countries.

### 19\. PIL by Cardtonic (Nigeria)

![PIL product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/cardtonic/screenshot.webp)

[PIL](https://liners.com/cardtonic) is a business spend management platform built by Cardtonic.

**The problem:** Naira cards are regularly declined on Meta ads, SaaS subscriptions and cloud bills.

### 20\. Startbutton (Nigeria)

![Startbutton product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/startbutton/screenshot.webp)

[Startbutton](https://liners.com/startbutton) is a merchant of record for foreign digital businesses selling in Africa.

**The problem:** A foreign app can't collect local payments in African markets without setting up a local company, tax registration and licences in each one.

**The solution:** Local collections, tax compliance, incorporation and payouts in about 15 markets.

### 21\. iRecharge and Plug.ng (Nigeria)

![Plug.ng product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/plug/screenshot.webp)

![iRecharge product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/content/inline/6d467894-d4c3-4586-aef8-72559d51be31.webp?v=1793547804622)

[iRecharge](https://irecharge.ng/?utm_source=liners.com) and [Plug.ng](https://liners.com/plug-ng) are bill payment apps.

## Banking, Savings, Credit and Wealth Companies at Moonshot 2026

The second-biggest group at Moonshot 2026 was building around one fear: watching your money lose value as the Naira falls. That has turned savings apps, dollar investments and fast credit into everyday tools, and the companies here range from a billion-dollar business bank to a startup that lends against your smartphone.

### 22\. Moniepoint (Nigeria)

![Moniepoint product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/moniepoint/screenshot.webp)

[Moniepoint](https://liners.com/moniepoint) is a payments and banking platform for small businesses, founded in 2015 by Tosin Eniolorunda.

**The problem:** Most Nigerian retailers can't get working-capital loans from traditional banks, and they run payments, banking and bookkeeping on separate tools.

**The solution:** POS terminals, business and personal accounts, expense cards, bookkeeping and inventory loans. Moniepoint processes $17 billion a month across 10 million+ accounts and closed a $200 million Series C in 2025.

### 23\. PiggyVest (Nigeria)

![PiggyVest product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/piggyvest/screenshot.webp)

[PiggyVest](https://liners.com/piggyvest) is a savings and investment app with over 6 million customers. It started in 2016 as Piggybank.ng.

**The problem:** Money kept in a regular bank account earns little interest while inflation eats into it, and easy access makes it tempting to spend.

**The solution:** Automatic savings, locked savings that can't be withdrawn early (Safelock), target savings, dollar savings and investments. PiggyVest paid out ₦1.3 trillion to users in 2025.

### 24\. Risevest (Nigeria)

![Risevest product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/content/inline/22eb1ff3-f5cc-4edf-80ec-b5dadbbd52f1.webp?v=1793547780863)

[Risevest](https://liners.com/risevest) is a dollar investment app that manages portfolios for Nigerians.

**The problem:** Savings held in Naira lose value as the currency falls, and buying US stocks or property from Nigeria is complicated.

**The solution:** Managed dollar portfolios of US stocks, real estate and fixed income. Rise has 850,000+ users across Rise and Chaka, and holds an SEC fund manager licence.

### 25\. Kredete (Nigeria / USA)

![Kredete product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/kredete/screenshot.webp)

[Kredete](https://liners.com/kredete) is a remittance app that also helps African immigrants build a US credit score, founded in 2023 by Adeola Adedewe.

**The problem:** African immigrants arrive in the US with no credit history, so they're refused loans and apartments, while also paying high fees to send money home.

**The solution:** Transfers to 60+ markets combined with credit-building and a stablecoin-backed credit card. Kredete raised a $22 million Series A and has 700,000+ monthly users.

### 26\. Platnova (Nigeria)

![Platnova product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/platnova/screenshot.webp)

[Platnova](https://liners.com/platnova) is a multicurrency money app for individuals and businesses.

**The problem:** Paying overseas tuition, receiving money from abroad or sending money to family in another country often means using several apps and losing money on each conversion.

**The solution:** Global transfers, cards, savings, tuition payments and invoicing across 13+ currencies and 50+ countries.

### 27\. Accrue (Nigeria / Ghana)

![Accrue product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/useaccrue/screenshot.webp)

[Accrue](https://liners.com/accrue) is a cross-border payments and dollar account app, founded in 2021 by Adesuwa Omoruyi and Clinton Mbah.

**The problem:** Sending money between African countries, say from Ghana to Nigeria, is slow and expensive through banks.

**The solution:** Intra-African transfers through 16,000 verified agents, plus USD accounts and savings across 15+ countries. Accrue raised a $1.58 million seed.

### 28\. Nefer (Nigeria / Canada)

![Nefer product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/usenefer/screenshot.webp)

[Nefer](https://liners.com/nefer) is a Canada-Nigeria payments app.

**The problem:** Nigerians paying Canadian tuition or bidding on US car auctions have to source CAD and USD through several middlemen.

**The solution:** Transfers and payments for tuition, bills and car auctions like Copart and IAAI. Nefer has processed over C$100 million.

[Belema](https://liners.com/belema) is a CBN-licensed payment switch and core banking provider for banks and fintechs.

**The solution:** Belema Switch and Belcoba, a plug-and-play core banking and payments system that goes live in a day.

### 32\. Credlock Africa (Nigeria)

What if your phone was your collateral?

**The solution:** Loans of 3 to 6 months worth up to 50% of a phone's value. The phone stays with the user, and an Android lock restricts it if repayments lapse. Credlock has lent about ₦1.5 billion across 33 states.

### 34\. Scrella (Nigeria)

![Scrella product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/scrella/screenshot.webp)

[Scrella](https://liners.com/scrella) is an insurtech for phones and devices, founded in 2023 by Gbenga Adigun.

**The problem:** Replacing a stolen or broken phone is expensive, and traditional insurers require a physical inspection before they'll cover it.

**The solution:** Insurance for new and used phones, with AI remote inspection through the app.

### 35\. Centrave (Nigeria)

![Centrave product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/content/inline/ac37d8c2-1773-476a-a102-1664efc805ae.webp?v=1793547816931)

[Centrave](https://centrave.group/?utm_source=liners.com) is a financial services group that currently runs GreenAcres Microfinance Bank.

**The problem:** Customers use a different provider for banking, insurance, pensions and investments.

**The solution:** One group covering banking, savings and credit through GreenAcres, with remittance, insurance and pensions planned.

## Crypto and Digital Asset Companies at Moonshot 2026

The crypto companies at Moonshot 2026 weren't selling moonshots (pun intended). Most of them solve one practical problem: turning crypto, gift cards and stablecoins into Naira you can spend, or dollars you can hold, without the scams and delays of peer-to-peer trading.

### 36\. Breet (Nigeria / Ghana)

![Breet product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/breet/screenshot.webp)

[Breet](https://liners.com/breet) is a crypto payments platform that converts crypto into local currency, with 500,000+ verified users.

**The problem:** Cashing out crypto through peer-to-peer traders is slow and risky; buyers can disappear or send fake payment screenshots.

**The solution:** Direct crypto-to-bank payouts in Nigeria and Ghana, plus an OTC desk, crypto cards, bill payments and a developer API.

### 37\. Obiex (Nigeria)

![Obiex product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/obiex/screenshot.webp)

[Obiex](https://liners.com/obiex) is a zero-fee crypto trading and swap platform that also supplies liquidity to other exchanges.

**The problem:** Retail users pay high spreads on Naira crypto trades, and smaller exchanges don't have enough liquidity to fill orders quickly.

**The solution:** Instant buy, sell and swap with usernames instead of wallet addresses, plus a liquidity API used by about 12 Nigerian exchanges. Obiex reported $731.5 million in annualised trade volume and says it's profitable.

### 38\. Monica (Nigeria)

![Monica product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/monica/screenshot.webp)

[Monica](https://liners.com/monica) is a crypto-to-Naira off-ramp app with 700,000+ users.

**The problem:** Selling BTC or USDT through peer-to-peer traders can take hours, and rates often change before the Naira arrives.

**The solution:** Payouts for seven coins to any Nigerian bank in about 60 seconds, plus bill payments, a virtual dollar card and an off-ramp API.

### 39\. Busha (Nigeria)

![Busha product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/busha/screenshot.webp)

[Busha](https://liners.com/busha) is a crypto exchange whose Busha Business product gives companies stablecoin and dollar infrastructure.

**The problem:** African businesses that need to pay foreign invoices can't get dollars from banks in time.

**The solution:** Stablecoin on- and off-ramps, USD accounts, corporate cards and OTC across seven African currencies. Busha received provisional SEC approval as a digital asset exchange in 2024.

### 40\. Luno (Africa / Global)

![Luno product screenshot](https://assets.liners.com/storage/v1/object/public/media/tools/luno/screenshot.webp)

[Luno](https://liners.com/luno) is a crypto exchange and investment app that has served Nigeria since 2015.

**The problem:** Most Nigerians buy crypto through unregulated traders, where there's no recourse if a trade goes wrong or funds are frozen.

**The solution:** A regulated exchange to buy, sell and store crypto. In July 2026, Luno became the first global exchange admitted to the Nigerian SEC's ARIP programme.

### 41\. Prestmit (Nigeria)

## What Moonshot 2026 Tells Us About Where African Tech Is Heading

Put almost 100 companies side by side and six patterns show up. Most of them are fixing basic infrastructure: how money crosses borders, how small businesses run day to day, and how people protect their savings from a falling Naira.

**1\. Cross-border money is the problem of the decade;** Grey, Raenest, Juicyway, Esca, HoneyCoin, Nefer, Timon and over a dozen others exist because getting paid from abroad, paying a foreign supplier or sending money to another African country is still slow and expensive.

**2\. Stablecoins went mainstream, and nobody calls it crypto anymore;** HoneyCoin, Daya, Busha, Kredete and PIL settle transactions in stablecoins, while the customer sees a dollar account or a card.

**3\. Protecting savings from inflation is its own category;** PiggyVest, Risevest and the dollar-account apps all help people move money out of Naira or earn returns that outpace inflation.

**4\. AI is being built for African realities;** Aethex trains voice models on African accents, Lena follows the Nigerian curriculum, JusticePadi explains Nigerian rights on WhatsApp, and BrandDrive handles FIRS e-invoicing. The AI isn't generic; it's local.

**5\. Energy is everyone's problem;** Acecore, Earthbond, Manamuz and Rivy all sell or finance solar, and Acecore winning TC Battlefield shows how urgent the judges rated it.

**6\. WhatsApp is the new app store;** JusticePadi, MyPadi, Demfati's Deeva and LincRide all serve users inside WhatsApp, because that's where Africans already spend their day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

What is Moonshot by TechCabal?▾

Moonshot is TechCabal's annual African tech conference. The 2026 edition, its fourth, ran on 28 and 29 October at the National Theatre in Lagos under the theme "Courage & Conviction: Building for a New World", with Grey as headline sponsor.

How many people attended Moonshot 2026?▾

Over 7,500 people attended across two days, up from about 6,000 in 2025.

Who won TC Battlefield at Moonshot 2026?▾

Acecore, a Nigerian climate-tech startup building locally made solar battery systems, won $10,000. Supplya came second with $5,000 and Navidyn Limited placed third with $2,500.

Which sector had the most companies at Moonshot 2026?▾

Payments and cross-border money, with over 20 companies including Flutterwave, Paystack, Interswitch, OPay, Grey, Raenest and Juicyway. Banking, savings and credit came second.

Were all the companies at Moonshot 2026 Nigerian?▾

Most were Nigerian, but not all. HoneyCoin is from Kenya, Swiftway Shippers is from Ghana, Accrue operates from Accra and Lagos, and several Nigerian-founded companies are registered in the US, UK, Canada or Ireland.
